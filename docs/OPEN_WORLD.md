# Open World

A 3x3-block neighbourhood ("Moore Park") with a city-scale society behind it: named residents with homes, jobs, moods and a
life log walk the sidewalks, animals trot between yards, and the player's car can run them down. The scene runs solo
(`#scene=openworld`) and as a room's game (kind `openworld`), where every peer sees the same crowd and the proximity voice works
as everywhere. Nothing here is a replay concern: civilians and their hits live in this scene's course only.

## The brain (`ai/civilians`)

One society and its crowd, both pure functions of (seed, crowd clock, host events), computed by modules the main thread and a
Web Worker both import.

- **Macro: the society.** Every resident has a name, gender, age, a home, a job at a workplace, a partner and children, a mood and a
  life log ring. A society tick per game hour applies due events; at each game-day boundary the daily chances run (jobs,
  retirement, firing, promotion, courtship, babies, moving, injury, death and replacement), each drawn from a stream keyed by
  (seed, resident, day).
- **Micro: the crowd.** Needs (rest, food, work, social, errand, solitude) rise over the day and fall while dwelling; at the end of a
  dwell a resident picks a place by urgency x time-of-day weight / distance, plans a path on the walk graph (A*, edge costs raised by
  the city's fear of a corner) and walks it. A leg is (depart tick, place, node path, speed, lateral offset).
- **Clock.** Decisions fall on a 2 Hz grid of crowd time (`DECISION_HZ`); a crowd second is a game minute, a day is 24 real minutes,
  and the start hour comes from the seed.
- **Feedback.** Host events change the society: a `knock` (a civilian run down: hospital for half a game day, hurt, the corner
  feared), a `wreck` (a building hit hard: its place closes for two game days, its staff are laid off), and the street's own `crime`,
  `bust`, `push` and `fight` (see "Interactions, crime and seats"). An event at time `t` changes the civilian it names at once, everyone
  drawn into it (witnesses, a fight's two, a crime's victims) `EVENT_LEAD_TICKS` after it, and the rest of the society at the first hour
  tick after that, so a peer that hears it up to that late applies it on the same tick as the host.

`CivilianRemote` is the main thread's end: it posts one plan per tick `PLAN_LEAD_TICKS` ahead to the worker and mirrors the city;
a tick whose answer is not back when due is computed inline by the same step.

## The crowd on the main thread (`CivilianCrowd`)

`CivilianCrowd(map, seed, playableResidents, remote)` owns a `CrowdPoses` (what the drawing reads: drawn position, yaw, stride phase,
speed, look word, shown flag, and the activity the society or a bump gives each: code, clock, time left, partner, feet and surface height). `step(crowdSeconds, cars, authority)` runs once per sim step on the host and solo player and once
per frame on a client:

1. every decision tick passed is advanced through the brain (on the host, `CrimeDesk.collect` hands the crimes now due to the log as
   `crime` events);
2. each resident on a leg is placed by `legPositionAt` at the crowd time (a pure function of the rows and the time: any frame rate,
   no integration, no allocation); the stride phase is the metres walked, so it needs no state. A resident in a car on a leg by car
   is not shown (the traffic draws it); one the society places (`readActivity`: seated, at a counter, fighting) is shown where it says;
3. the shown are kept a body apart and their bumps found (below);
4. with `authority`, every shown person is tested against every car (`runsDown` within `CAR_REACH`, `looseRunsDown` for a car with
   a torn part flying): a hit hides them at once, logs a `knock` event and queues a strike for the ragdoll throw, dressed as the drawn crowd dresses
   them (`dressOfLook` of their look word, the same dress the near dummy wears).

**Bodies.** Civilians keep a body's width apart (`BODY_GAP`, 0.6 m) without the brain knowing. `CrowdSeparation` (`civilian-separation.ts`) takes the brain's
spots, pairs the bodies by a hashed grid and relaxes the pairs `SEPARATION_PASSES` times, no body moving more than `MAX_OFFSET` (0.7 m) from the
brain's spot; a pair closing head on or overtaking is moved sideways instead (the side they were already on, so it never flips as they cross), so they pass instead of standing off. It is a
pure function of the brain's spots at that moment (no history, no allocation): every peer draws the same, and the brain's rows and the twin row never see it.
`poses.x`/`z` are the drawn spots (the art, the look-at, the ragdoll throw's spawn and the cars' hits read them: a car meets the body the player sees). Hits are found on the host only and a client hears
the knock as an event, so the host's drawn spots are the authority (the separation is a pure function of the brain's spots and the fixed bodies); a client's walker shifts only its own drawn spots, never a hit.
`crowd.overlay` (the law's: the fleeing suspect) runs after the placing and before the hits, so a hit meets the suspect where they are drawn. A bump or push is the brain walking two bodies through each other, so it is
found on the brain's spots, which the drawn spots no longer show. The drawn spots a hit meets include the players' walkers (immovable bodies the civilians are pushed round), so a future reel of open-world hits must record the walker positions as well as the crowd's clock. A player on foot is a body that does not give way (`crowd.walkers.add`, which the director refills each step from the `foot` port), and so is a foot cop (`addBody`: civilians step round and bump him, but his contact is no push event);
a civilian seated, at a counter or fighting is fixed too, and is not bumped.

**Bumps and pushes.** Two civilians whose brain spots come within `BUMP_DISTANCE` closing at `BUMP_CLOSING_SPEED` bump: both play the bump clip (`ACTIVITY.bump`,
partners of each other) for its length, at most once in `BUMP_COOLDOWN_SECONDS`; every peer finds this itself. On the host only, a hard collision (`HARD_DISTANCE`,
`HARD_CLOSING_SPEED`), a player on foot walking into a civilian (`WALKER_PUSH_DISTANCE`), a car's side passing within `CAR_NUDGE_GAP` of one at `CAR_NUDGE_SPEED` or more,
and every knock, is a `push` event: a triple whose subject is the victim and, after it with the same seconds, an `alongside` triple whose subject is the civilian who pushed, or a `walker` triple whose
subject is the bearing from the victim's brain spot to the player who shoved (`WALKER_BEARING_STEPS` a turn; the brain turns a fight his way); a car's nudge or a car's knock has neither (a fight's knock-out names its striker in an `alongside` triple after its push: `findPunches`): the wire's subject is 16 bits, so a pair is never packed into one. At most one per resident per `PUSH_LOCKOUT_SECONDS`; a knock logs its `knock` first and its push after. The brain rolls the fight from a push; nothing here changes its rows.

**At the car.** A resident the society has getting into or out of her car (`ACTIVITY.carIn`: the clip's seconds before the car leaves, standing at its node; `carOut`: the first seconds after it is parked) is shown fixed at the door spot `crowd.driverDoors(resident, code, out)` fills: world x, z and yaw, her feet's height and her seat top's. The director's provider (`engine-openworld-doors.ts`, on every peer that has the traffic's ports) is the owner's car (`carOwner`: a housemate may be the one at the door) in the parking space the traffic picks for the owner at the kerb of `carPlace` (getting in) or `carTo` (getting out) (`residentCarPose`), the real body's seat (`carDoorSpot` of `CAR_DOOR`) and the real ground, all pure in the brain's rows and the space, so every peer fills the same; none set, or no car, and she is not drawn. She is a fixed body (civilians are drawn round her) and her clip's clock and end come from the reader. `OpenWorldCarDoors` (host and solo) swings the owner's car door by the clip. `activityReader` is the seam a row uses to give a resident a word the brain does not say yet.

**Other host events.** `raiseEvent(kind, subject, seconds?)` puts one more host event in the log and the brain's next plan (a bust, from the cops). The crowd itself raises, on the host only: a `danger` (subject: the walk-graph node nearest) for a car at `DANGER_SPEED` heading at a civilian within `DANGER_RANGE` who is still on their feet `DANGER_CONFIRM_SECONDS` on, at most one a resident in `DANGER_LOCKOUT_SECONDS`, and for a car's hard hit on something that is no building's within `CRASH_NODE_REACH` of a street node, at most one in `CRASH_LOCKOUT_SECONDS`; and an `ambulance` (subject: the person) `AMBULANCE_DELAY_SECONDS` after someone is run down, with `onAmbulanceCall(x, z, seconds)` for whoever sends the ambulance (the director gives the traffic's `callAmbulance`).

Cats and dogs live in the brain (they decide, walk and keep a life log) but the crowd shows none until there is animal art: a
resident of species other than person has `shown = 0` in every pose, so none is drawn and none can be run down.

A civilian's **look word** (`LOOK` in `civilian-crowd-constants.ts`) is a hash of who they are (bits 0-28), their gender (bit 29,
so a drawn Rosa is a woman) and their species (bits 30-31: person, cat, dog). The drawing dresses from it; the species bits are there for the animal art to
read.

**Wrecks.** The cheapest existing signal is the course's solid-prop hit, `RaceField.onWallHit(car, closing, x, z)` (every
solid a car meets, with its closing speed and contact point). The open world wraps it: a hit at `WRECK_SPEED` or more whose point
lies on the footprint (grown by `WRECK_MARGIN`) of a place inside the walls is a `wreck` event for that place, at most one per
`WRECK_LOCKOUT_SECONDS` per place. The wrap is let go when the course is left.

## Determinism split

| Piece | Where it is decided |
|---|---|
| Society rows, needs, decisions, legs | deterministic: seed + crowd clock + event log; the worker computes, the main thread mirrors and computes any tick whose answer is late |
| A civilian's position at time `t` | pure function of the leg and `t` on the main thread |
| Knock, wreck | host-authoritative events: the host detects in its sim step, gives them to its brain at once and sends them |
| Game clock and seed | the host's: a client runs the host's crowd time on its own clock |
| The ragdoll of a knocked civilian | local presentation, thrown from the strike (the host's own, a client's from the car nearest the civilian when its clock reaches the knock) |
| Stride, look word | local presentation (still a pure function of the rows) |
| Drawn spot (separation), bump clip | local presentation: a pure function of the brain's spots at the crowd time |
| Push events | host: found on the brain's spots, logged and sent like knocks |

The twin row (`civilian-crowd-twin.test.ts`) steps one crowd with the real worker, one with none and one whose answers all come after
their ticks were taken, with a knock and a wreck heard at fixed frames: poses and society rows are bit for bit equal at every frame.

## Wire

`MSG.crowd` (`net/crowd-codec.ts`) carries `{seed, hostTime, crowdTime, events}`, reliably, with every keyframe and at once when the
host's log grew. The **seed** is the crowd's key: the population tier (phone 60, desktop 150, ultra 300 residents, `POPULATION`) above
the 24-bit scene seed, so a client builds the host's city size without a new field. The event log is the newest 512 (kind, subject,
crowd seconds) triples (`EVENT_LOG_CAPACITY`, equal to the wire's cap). A push or fight names its victim, and a civilian on the other side
in an `alongside` triple of the same seconds right after it (none: a player's walker or car), so a subject stays under 16 bits.

A client has no crowd until it hears a message. It runs the host's crowd time on its own clock the way the snapshot clock does (the
least local-minus-host gap seen, a 1 ms creep up per message), `INTERP_DELAY` behind, where the cars it draws are. A client that
joins late replays the log's events at the ticks they happened in, so its society equals the host's; an event ahead of its clock waits
for it. The brain is told of a heard event by posting it with the next plan tick not yet asked of the worker.

## Look-at

`lookAtOf` (`civilian-look.ts`) names what the eye rests on: the shown civilian whose chest the eye's forward ray passes within
`LOOK_RADIUS`, up to `LOOK_REACH` ahead (their line: name, age, job at workplace, need and place, mood; then their life log), else the
building whose box the ray enters first (its kind and name, who lives or works there, the latest comings and goings), whichever is
nearer along the ray. The director recomputes it `LOOK_HZ` times a second and keeps the same object while the text is the same; the
HUD's look panel shows it.

## Interactions, crime and seats (`ai/civilians/society-*.ts`)

**The table** (`society-table.ts`, `INTERACTIONS`) answers what a resident does where, from mood x job x place x hour. A row applies when the
resident has one of its jobs (employed, jobless, broke, retired, adult, criminal, wanted), all its traits (coffee fan, drinker), a mood
it lists, stands where it says (home, or at work after a shift's dwell), in its hours, with its need high enough. It is a candidate beside
the needs, worth `strength` times the need's level, falling with distance and fear like any destination. Rows: coffee on the way to work
and after waking out of work, a drink after a shift when tired and a drink to forget when sad or angry, sitting out on the stoop (evening;
by day for the retired and jobless), a chat with a neighbour (who comes out to their own door when the visitor arrives), a row with one
when angry, loitering at a shop door when broke, lifting from a shop, mugging someone sitting out, trying parked cars, lying low while wanted.
Named residents keep their stories and are not in the table; children and animals are not either.

**Venues and hours** (`society-venues.ts`): a place is a coffee shop, bar, diner, shop, supermarket, office, home, park or stop, from its
`interior`, its facade style or its kind. Opening hours come from the map (`Place.hours`), else a default by venue (coffee 6-15, bars from
16, shops 7-22, supermarket 8-21); an hour outside them weighs `CLOSED_HOURS_WEIGHT`, so coffee is chosen in the morning, shops by day, bars
at dusk and night. Every destination, a need's or a row's, is ranked by that weight over distance. The police station (`Moore St Police Station`, `PLACE_NAMES.policeStation`: two lots on the south face of block B3, an office's facade and interior, sector `police`, open at every hour) is a private place (`PRIVATE_PLACE_NAMES`): no one works there, visits it or picks it as a hangout; it is only where the arrested are held.

**Seats** (`society-seats.ts`): a customer at a coffee shop, bar or diner takes a free seat from `Place.seats` (one holder per seat, held from
the moment they set out, freed when they leave or are knocked, busted or die); a resident sitting out takes one of the home's leading
`stoopSeats`. The holder is in their row (`seat`); the owner table is derived and kept equal by the mirror. A venue with a counter (`Place.counter`) also has `STAND_SPOTS` standing spots for customers who have no seat, numbered after its seats in the same table (one holder each, rows of `STAND_COLUMNS` behind the buying lane, `STAND_SPACING` apart: more than a body's width, so they are fixed bodies that need no separating), and `CLERK_SPOTS` along the clerk's spot for staff. A customer who is not held to a spot (a stop too short to sit or stand for) buys on a lane at the counter itself, in front of every standing spot, which the crowd keeps them apart on; a seated customer buys there first, then sits; someone who finds every spot taken is not drawn inside.

**Activity** (`society-activity.ts`): `readActivity` is a pure function of the row and the time that says what the drawing animates
(`civilian-activity.ts`'s codes), since when and until when, with whom, the surface they sit at, and, for someone the brain places (seated,
at a counter, at a door, fighting), where. Someone in a car is nobody on foot; a door clip runs on the first and last second of a walk out of or into
a building, and getting into or out of a car is a stationary `carIn` or `carOut` clip at the car's node.

**Cars** (`civilian-plan.ts`, `society-cars.ts`): about a third of adults own a car (`FLAG.hasCar`), standing at the kerb of the place it was last parked at (`carPlace`,
`carTo`, `carLeaveTick`, `carParkTick` on the owner's row: before its trip leaves it stands where it was, once parked at the trip's end, in between it is away, and nobody
else's to take from the decision on). A trip for work, home, an errand, a meal, company, a coffee or a drink of at least `DRIVE_MIN_BLOCKS` blocks (`BLOCK_WALK_METRES` each)
is made by car when the resident's own car, or a housemate's, is free and within `CAR_WALK_METRES` of where they are (`chooseCar`): the leg is one path, the walk from the door to the
node beside the car's door (`MapIndex.carNode`: the end of a door's spur to the sidewalk), the way by the graph to the node beside the destination's door, and the walk in. The
driver walks to the car, stands there getting in (`carIn`), is not seen while the car drives (`DRIVE_SPEED` on the graph's metres, `PARK_SECONDS` to park), stands at the parked
car getting out (`carOut`) and walks in. The row carries the path indices where they get in and out and the ticks (`driveFirst`, `driveLast`, `driveLeaveTick`, `driveParkTick`,
`carOwner`), so `legPose` is still a pure function of the row and the time. A knock, arrest or death before they get in gives the trip up (the car stays); after, it stands.
`fillCommuterFeed` lists each car on the road from the tick its driver gets in to the tick it is parked (the owner is the resident listed) and each car that stands, with its kerb place.

**Crime** (`society-crime.ts`, `society-reactions.ts`): each game day a working-age resident's chance of turning to crime adds a base, being out of
work, broke (three jobless days), angry and young (`CRIME_LEAN`); criminals give it up at `REFORM_CHANCE` a day (twice with a job), and a city starts
at the share those chances settle to. A criminal's table row takes them to a shop, a stoop sitter or a parked car; the brain commits nothing there
by itself: the host's `CrimeDesk` lists those who have been at the place `CRIME_LAG_TICKS`, and the host raises `EVENT_KIND.crime`. It makes the criminal
wanted a game day from the tick it happened, fears the corner, angers the clerk or owner, and sends witnesses at their doors to report it or run home.
`EVENT_KIND.bust` (raised by the police) takes the wanted or fighting resident off the street for `JAIL_TICKS`, clears their crime and fight, and holds them in the police station (`placeByName`; at home on a map without one), their dwell there ending when the jail time is up: they are let out of its door (`decideResident` sends a resident let out of custody away from home straight home, `goHomeFromCustody`).
Rows (`crimeTick`, `crimeKind`, `crimePlace`, `crimeVictim`, `fightTick`, `fightPartner`, flags) carry it all to peers and the police.

**Crossings** (`society-crossing.ts`, `civilian-signals.ts`): with the city's lights (`CivilianCrowd`'s `signals`: the open world passes them, the tests' fixture maps do not), a walker who comes to a crossing waits at the kerb node until the cars of the street they cross are red and will stay red while they cross (`PedestrianSignals.waitSeconds`, the traffic's own `lightState`), up to `MAX_WAITS` times in a leg; the leg takes that much longer (the waits are in the row: `waitCount`, `waitIndex0..2`, `waitTicks0..2`), and `legPose` holds them at the node while it lasts (`LegPose.waiting`, speed 0). A walker goes against the light with the seeded chance `CROSS_DART_BASE` + `CROSS_DART_JUMPY` x their neuroticism, for each crossing.

**Fights**: `EVENT_KIND.push` rolls a seeded chance (`FIGHT_CHANCE`, times `FIGHT_ANGRY_FACTOR` when either civilian is angry) that the pushed civilian comes to blows with
whoever pushed: another civilian, a car, or, when a `walker` triple follows, the human player on foot; `EVENT_KIND.fight` makes it so. A push by a body that is not a civilian names the way to it in a triple of the same seconds after it (`walker`: the player on foot, `car`: a car that nudged them, in `WALKER_BEARING_STEPS` of a turn), and the one pushed squares up facing it (against a car, with no one as the other fighter). Fighters stop at the nearest door for
`FIGHT_TICKS`. Two civilians face each other and are both wanted for it; one who turns on the player faces the walker's bearing (`fightPartner` is `FIGHT_PLAYER`), is wanted for it,
and swings at him: a swing lands half way through its clip, and when the host's walker is within `PLAYER_STRIKE_REACH` of the fighter's spot the director knocks him down the way the
walker is knocked down already (`findWalkerStrike`, the `strikeWalker` port), with no damage beyond it. A civilian run down may be taken on by a bystander standing at a door.
`EVENT_KIND.jaywalk` (raised by the police for a walker crossing against the light) holds a free resident `JAYWALK_CUSTODY_TICKS` and sends them home, annoyed.
In 60 % of fights between two civilians, the starter's blow number 2, 3, 4 or 5 (one seeded draw, `STREAM.punch`, per victim and fight: `knockoutBlow`)
knocks the other out: the host's `findPunches` fells her as a car's knock does (`knockDown` with the striker named: knock, push, then an `alongside` triple), the striker's fight ends (`releaseFightPartner`), and a client
throws her along the blow from the drawn spots. She is then in hospital and walks home like anyone knocked down.

A bust, knock or jaywalk arrest hides its resident from the event's tick for its whole window on every peer: the step that applies it never lets them back early, since a mirror
that only applies the worker's answers never runs that step.

## What a client sees of the host's scenes

A client has no traffic: its cars are the host's, streamed as sim cars. Three scenes that the traffic drives on the host are told to it another way.

- **The ambulance.** The host notes each change of the ambulance's state or car in the crowd's event log as an `ambulanceStage` event (subject: car index times 4 plus the state; `engine/engine-openworld-ambulance.ts`). The log is sent to every guest whenever it grows, and the brain drops the kind. A client reads the newest stage: its medics kneel where the person down lay (the strike its own crowd threw at the knock the call names; the ambulance's car if it never saw it) while it is loading, and the Auto camera follows the ambulance's car. No wire layout changes: the one new thing is the kind value 12 in the log's kind byte.
- **Arrests in patrol cars.** A client's own law has no patrol car, so it walks an arrested pair to the station on foot. The host logs the pair's boarding as an `arrestRide` event and their getting out at the station as an `arrestAlight` (`OpenWorldLaw.logRide`): two entries of the kind at one crowd second, the resident (`ARREST_PLAYER_RESIDENT` for the player on foot's arrest), then the car's index in the engine's list. The brain drops both kinds. A client reads them once a frame, each once its own law has reached the event's second (`ArrestRides.hear`, `engine/engine-openworld-arrest-rides.ts`, a stand-in for `PatrolCars` over the engine's cars): on a ride, the suspect it is leading away is seated in that car with her cop, off the street (`Arrests.rideHeard`); on an alighting they get out at the car's door, its door swinging, and walk in at the station as on the host. A car the client lacks, the player's own arrest, or a pair it is not leading away change nothing. The wire layout is unchanged: kinds 14 and 15 in the kind byte (`NET_VERSION` 60).
- **The driver's doors.** A door is a part of a sim car, and a part's state rides a snapshot only for a crashed car, so a client's civilian reached for a door that stayed shut. `engine-openworld-door-clips.ts` opens the driver's door of the car whose door the civilian stands at, at the angle of her clip (`carDoorOpenness`), and shuts it when nobody is at it. It is local drawing; the host's swing of the sim door (`OpenWorldCarDoors`) is unchanged.
- **A driver at the wheel.** A thrown-out driver is hidden on every peer (the snapshot carries `driverOut`), and a car standing in a kerb space has none (`carIsEmpty`). What a client cannot know is that a peer's player has left his car on foot: nothing on the wire says so (see the walker message in the gap list).

## The Auto camera

The camera key (V, T, C, B on a pad) steps the driven car's views and, in the open world, one more after the hood view: Auto, then back to the chase. Auto follows one subject at a time, a civilian, a foot cop, a resident or patrol driver, or the spot of a wreck or a knock-down, and its top-left bubble is that subject's own look-at (the line and life log the panel shows when the eye is close; a driver's names the wheel; a patrol car's says what it is doing). The speed gauge is down while it is up.

**Steering it.** A mouse drag or pointer-lock mouse look turns the view round the subject, the one orbit every followed thing has, whatever it is (a person, a foot cop, a resident driving, a patrol car, the ambulance): `ChaseCamera`'s ride-along hold (`rideHeld`, `frameRide`, `orbit`, the same drag that turns a thrown driver's ride), so there is no subject the mouse works on and another it does not. The follow goes on while you turn it, and `RIDE_PAUSE` (2.5 s) after you let go the Auto shot picks up from where the camera stands. Q / E (LB / RB) go to the previous / next person, foot cop or driver, as when spectating a race: the subjects by distance from the one on screen, nearest first, kept between presses (6 s) so Q undoes E. **Auto resumes** `MANUAL_HOLD_SECONDS` (30 s) after the last pick or turn: until then the director makes no cut and no interruption, then it cuts on; the subject going (out of sight, into a building) ends the hold at once. The pad's right stick and the wheel do not turn it.

`ai/openworld-auto/`: `AutoDirector` (pure) holds a subject a seeded 5 to 10 seconds, then cuts to the best other one: civilians by what they are doing (a fight, an arrest, a bump), their mood and what the crowd's event log says is in progress (a crime, a bust, a knock, a push, a jaywalker), wanted residents, foot cops (a run is a pursuit), cars with a driver (a lit siren first, then by speed), wrecks and knock-downs as spots, a driver getting out of his parked car, a witness on the phone to the police and someone kneeling by the hurt, and the ambulance (loading at the scene first, on its way next). The bubble adds what a civilian is doing (a scene's own words) above his life log. It never cuts to the subject on screen or the one it left (unless nothing else is there), counts a repeat of the kind on screen and of the last six subjects less, and once a shot has held 5 seconds an event in progress that beats it by a margin cuts at once. `OpenWorldScan` reads the world for it; `present/openworld-auto-cam.ts` frames the subject over the shoulder or behind its car, turned a seeded way round, on springs, with the chase cams' wall clearance (`EyeClear`). Local to the viewer: wall time, nothing on the wire.

## HUD

The open world's free roam has no session, but the race HUD reads the driven (or followed) car as a race's: `RaceHudLayer.hud` gives it a view with no racer (`roamingView`), so the drive cluster Survival shows (speed, gear, revs, damage, boost, slide) is up and no place, lap, clock or bust strip is; on foot there is no view, so no gauge. The race view and the Full menu toggle (H) work as in a race: the race view hides the menus.

**Alarm and the ambulance** (`society-alarm.ts`): at a knock's, a wreck's or a danger's reaction tick every free person out within `ALARM_SIGHT` of the spot (standing at a door, or walking: not indoors, hurt, held, in a car or a named resident) reacts by their own seeded draw (`ALARM_SHARE`). A row remembers it (`alarmTick`, `alarmX`, `alarmZ`, `alarmVictim`); `readActivity` has them flinch for the recoil clip, a step of `RECOIL_STEP_METRES` back from it and facing it, whoever they are. One at a door then goes in (`ALARM_INDOORS_SHARE`) or looks on (`PURPOSE.lookOn`) for `LOOK_ON_CRASH_MINUTES`, or for `LOOK_ON_DOWNED_MINUTES` for someone down, glancing at them; a walker out on a road crossing within `EVADE_REACH` of it does not flinch where it stands: it clears the road to the nearer kerb (`legPose`: a pure function of the row and the time), ahead (it runs there and waits for its schedule to come level) or behind (it runs back, waits `EVADE_HOLD_SECONDS`, then runs to meet its schedule), at `EVADE_PACE_CALM` to `EVADE_PACE_JUMPY` times its pace by its neuroticism (`LegPose.speed` carries the pace the crowd draws it going at); any other walker walks on. At an `ambulance` event's reaction tick the nearest person out at a door within sight (not the one down) is on the phone for `CALL_MINUTES` (`PURPOSE.callAmbulance`, the phone-call clip, glancing at who is down). The one run down is already in hospital in the brain (`HOSPITAL_TICKS`): the ambulance and the person lying in the street are the traffic's and the ragdoll's.

## Seen and unseen (`SightField`)

`OpenWorldDirector.sight` (`present/openworld-sight.ts`, updated at the top of each frame) answers one question for everything that draws or streams: does a local eye see this? `seen(x, z, radius)` is true when the thing's column (the radius, 2 m tall, on flat ground) is inside some eye's view frustum and at least one of the lines from that eye to its top (through its middle and both sides across the view) clears every building box of the course (`hidingSight` of the course's solids: boxes 2.5 m or taller; the line's height against the roof counts, so a camera above sees over a block). A box an endpoint stands in never blocks that line. The boxes sit in a 16 m grid and each query walks the cells under its lines: no allocation, no per-id state.

The field also keeps a clock per id for those that want it: `claim(count)` hands out a private range, `observe(id, x, z, radius)` is `seen` plus the stamp, `unseenFor(id)` the seconds since it was last seen, and `HIDE_GRACE` (3 s) is the grace after which a consumer phases the thing out and releases what it holds. `near(x, z, radius)` is the host's keep-alive: a player's car within reach. Netplay: each peer culls only what it draws; the host never stops simulating something because `seen` is false, only when it is also not `near` a player. `sight.enabled = false` makes everything seen (the A/B arm with no culling).

**Cars** (`present/openworld-car-lod.ts`, driven by `engine/engine-openworld-cull.ts`): the distance detail runs three rungs nearer than the match ladder's while the open world is up (a desktop cuts small parts at 20 m and all but the body at 40 m), and a car no eye has seen for `HIDE_GRACE`, no player's car is within 40 m of and the camera does not follow is taken off the draw (`group.visible`, which the wheel, lamp, part and driver batches honour), and drawn again the frame it is seen. It never touches a car it did not hide, and lets go of all of them while a reel replay plays or the open world ends.

## Animation (`present/civilian-anim*.ts`)

A near dummy does what her brain says she does: `CrowdPoses.activity` is an `ACTIVITY` code (`ai/civilians/civilian-activity.ts`: idle, talk,
buy, door, sitDown, sit, standUp, bump, swing, block, hit, sitSteps, cuffing, cuffed, arresting, escorting; 0 is the walk). `activityClock` is the seconds since it began (the clip's own
time, so a civilian who comes into view mid-activity is already in it), `activityLeft` the seconds until it ends, `partner` the civilian a
bump, fight or talk is with (the clips turn toward or away from her), `feetY` and `surfaceY` the floor and the seat or counter top, so a
sitter lands on any seat height (`Place.seats`, a stoop step) and a customer's hand finds the counter.

`poseCivilian` fills one `ActivityPose` and `poseActivity` poses the dummy: the walk's channels (the guards' stride) first, then the clip's
tracks (`civilian-anim-clips.ts`, keyframes per channel: pelvis, torso, head, each arm and leg) blended over them by a weight that eases in
from the walk and out over the clip's last `blendOut` seconds, then two solves: a hand reaches a point (counter, door handle, a face) through the
torso's lean, and `plant` holds the feet on the floor as the pelvis sinks. A clip is authored for its main side, mirrored toward the partner's
side (bump, fight) or the civilian's handedness. Looping clips grow their fidgets from their rest pose over 1.5 s and start at an offset per
civilian, so a bar of sitters does not glance together. No frame allocates (doubles go through `scratch` slots, not arguments: a double passed to a call that is not inlined is boxed on the heap).

The walk is the guards' stride rounded off: the legs swing as a sine flattened toward a triangle so a foot on the ground goes back about as fast as she goes forward (it does not slide: the stride is
1.5 m of walk to a turn of phase, whatever the speed), the knee bends as the leg passes under her, the shoulders turn 0.1 rad against the hips with the head half turning back, the hips fall 3 cm as the legs
spread and the head nods twice a stride. A change of her pose that no stride or key makes (a clip cut off by the brain, a start from standing, a stop) does not snap: `PoseSmoothing` (one per
`CivilianArt`, per civilian) draws the old pose at once and pays the difference back with a time constant of 0.18 s; a pose that only moves on is drawn exactly, so nothing is delayed or flattened. Bits of
her look word give each person a pace (the clip's clock a tenth quicker or slower) and an amplitude (an eighth more or less on the lean, the head and the arms); none set is neither. A flinch (`recoil`)
is quicker and bigger the jumpier the person (`neuroticismOf`: 0.85 to 1.25 times the pace, 0.7 to 1.3 times the size), starts with the hands flung up and the body back, and comes down while she still looks.

The walk is drawn smoothed and scaled by pace; none of it reaches the brain or the wire. `PoseSmoothing.walk` draws her spot as the brain's through a low-pass (0.18 s, never over 0.3 m behind, a gap over 2 m taken at once),
her speed as the metres that drawn spot moves a second, her stride phase on by those metres (a whole stride a second at most past a walk: `RUN_STRIDES_PER_SECOND`, as the foot cops) and her facing turned at most 10 rad/s. `fillWalk` takes
`gait` = that speed over a walk's 1.5 m/s: leg swing, hip drop and shoulder twist scale by it (0.3 to 1.6; under 0.3 m/s of drawn speed the 0.3 floor goes, `LEG_FLOOR_ABOVE`, so she settles her legs as she stops), the arms' swing 0.3 to 1.8, the knee bend 0.6 + 0.4 gait, and above a walk she leans in (0.15 rad a unit of gait over 1); she stands below 0.05 m/s.
A far figure swings by the brain's pace over a walk's, to 1.6. On a stoop's door spur (`CrowdPoses.onSpur`, `CivilianArt.stoops`) `plantOnTreads` solves each leg again so its ankle stands on the tread under it, not on the line through the step.

**Stoops (`world/openworld/openworld-stoops.ts`).** A walker's height is `CrowdPoses.feetY`. `Stoops` is the height field of the rowhomes' stoops (`STOOP`: five risers of 0.18 m, 0.28 m treads, a 0.5 m landing, 1.6 m wide
about the door's `DOOR.across`; the door is the sill, 0.9 m; `hasStoop` says which places have one: a rowhome, not the modern infill): pure in the place, so every peer reads the same, with each riser climbed over 0.14 m so
her height follows the treads and does not jump. The door node of a home is on the landing and its spur to the sidewalk runs straight out along the stoop (`attachDoors`), so a resident who leaves appears at the sill's
height, goes down the steps with her feet on the treads and is at the street's height a step past the bottom one; one who comes home does the reverse and is gone at the door. No path point carries a height and the
brain and the wire are as they were. A stoop lifts only a walker whose walk now is its door's spur (`leadsToDoor`, from the nodes `LegPose.from` and `to` the crowd's `fillPoses` reads off the leg): the sidewalk's
lane, offset toward the facade, runs over the foot of the stoops, and a walker passing along it stays on the street. The crowd lifts those it draws (`liftWalkers`), not one the society stands at a seat, a counter or a
car (those are placed, not on a leg); cops and medics never use a door, so they are not lifted. The physical bodies (`openworld-bodies.ts`) keep their rule that a civilian above the street is drawn as a posed dummy, so
a body hands over to the dummy at the foot of a stoop and takes her back past it. The far figures and the look-at ray read `feetY` too. A walker's legs stride as on the flat while her root follows the steps, so her feet
float or sink a few centimetres on a riser (no stairs gait yet), and a stoop sitter's feet (a fixed seat) rest on the street, not the tread below. A walker on a sidewalk leg (not a door's spur) whose drawn spot falls on a stoop, or within 0.6 m of its foot, is drawn moved straight out from the facade until clear (`Stoops.keepOut`); the same holds for a suspect the law draws running or led away; the brain's spot does not move.

An arrest is two clips a side, set by the law (`ai/openworld-crime/crime-poses.ts`): the suspect is `cuffing` while gripped (her main arm
taken behind her back, then the other) and `cuffed` after (hands behind her back, head down; walking, her legs stride as the walk does: the
led-away walk); the cop is `arresting` (behind her, both hands on her wrists) and then `escorting` (his right hand on her left upper arm,
whichever hand he favours). `ARREST` is the pair's spacing (the cop `cuffBehind` behind her, she `escortAhead` ahead and `escortAcross` to
his right, both facing her way): the law places them by it and the cop's hands reach for where the cuffed pose puts her wrists and arm, so the
two need no partner index across their separate pose sets.

Getting into or out of a car is two stationary clips (`carIn`, `carOut`: `ai/civilians/civilian-car-door.ts` has the geometry and the door's timeline). She stands
on the door spot of the driver's door facing the car (`CAR_DOOR.outFromSeat` out from the seat's H-point, `behindSeat` back; `surfaceY` the H-point's height
less `hipOverSeat`): carIn has a hand on the outer handle as the door swings open, a step back, in and round and down onto the seat with the feet planted on the
cabin floor, and a hand on the inner handle to pull the door shut; carOut the reverse, ending with a shove on the outer panel. The hand follows the handle by the
same `carDoorOpenness(activity, clock)` the host sets the car's door by (`openness * CAR_DOOR.openRad`), so the hand and the door stay together.

A witness to a crash acts three more clips: `phoneCall` (a loop: the main hand at her ear, the other forearm across her waist, her head to what she calls about), `kneel`
(a loop easing in and out over 0.7 s: on one knee beside a person on the ground, leaning over them, her near hand on them) and `recoil` (0.9 s: a flinch back with both
hands up and her head round to the danger). `partner` is the person looked at: it may be a hidden civilian, only its last place is read.

## Traffic (`ai/openworld-traffic`)

Real cars of the field drive the streets, stop at signals and park in the kerb spaces. They are sim cars on the host (and solo) and reach a client in the host's snapshots like every car (about 30 B a car at 30 Hz while undamaged: 12 movers about 11 KB/s a client). The pool is the cars past the seated ones (`first` = highest seat + 1): `MOVER_CARS` drivers (desktop 12, phone 6, the last `PATROL_CARS` of them police bodies for `spawnDrivenCar(..., { urgent: true })`) and `KERB_CARS` standing cars (desktop 10, phone 4). Mover and kerb cars wear everyday bodies only (`EVERYDAY_BODIES`: sedan, hatchback, wagon, pickup; the fleet slot's coupe or monster is rebuilt as a seeded pick, any other body is kept). A peer that joins takes the lowest free id: if it is a pool car, traffic lets go of it (its lease is `lost`).

- **Roads** (`world/openworld/openworld-roads.ts`): the 4 x 4 grid of junctions from `LAYOUT`, two lanes a street, traffic on the right; `openworld-parking.ts`: the kerb spaces on the block side of each lane (6 m each, 6 m clear of a junction's box), `stays` marks the 70 % that hold a car for good. A resident's car takes one of the four empty spaces nearest his door (`kerbSpotsNear`), the one he is by number; a leased car's `dueSeconds` (the feed's `arriveSeconds`) stands it in its space when it is late and unseen. `driverDoorPoint` is where a driver gets out and in.
- **Signals** (`traffic-lights.ts`): four phases a junction (north-south through, left, east-west through, left), each a green, 3 s yellow and 1.5 s all red; `lightPhase(junction, crowdSeconds)` is a pure function of the crowd clock, staggered per junction, the same on every peer. - **Driving** (`trip-route.ts`, `traffic-driver.ts`): a trip is points along the lanes, the turns and the swing into a space (a Dijkstra over lanes); the driver is pure pursuit with the lowest of the cruise speed, the turn caps, the signal ahead (red, or a yellow it can stop for, holds it at the bar; a decision to stop sticks), the car ahead on the route and the end of the route.
- **Cars** (`mover-fleet.ts`): ambient cars dwell in a space, pull out, drive to a free space and park; leased cars (`OpenWorldTraffic.spawnDrivenCar(resident, fromSpot, toSpot, options?)`: a resident's commute, a patrol) take a given trip, `redirect(handle, toSpot)` re-plans a leased car's trip from where it is to another free space (a patrol following the roads after a moving car), and `handle.driver` replaces the trip driver. `kerb-cars.ts`: real cars standing in the spaces within 70 m of a player that no eye sees, put away when unseen and 40 m off.
- **Ambulance** (`ambulance.ts`): `callAmbulance(x, z, crowdSeconds)` sends one white police-body car (the last mover slots before the patrol cruisers, `AMBULANCE_CARS`) with its lights on from a far space (at least `AMBULANCE_DEPOT_METRES`, one no eye sees first) as an urgent trip to the free space nearest the scene; it stands there `AMBULANCE_LOAD_SECONDS` with its lights on, then drives to another far space and is put away (lights off) when it gets there or when no eye sees it and no player is near. `ambulanceState()` / `ambulanceSpot()` say where it is. One call at a time.
- **Cars of residents** (`traffic-look.ts`): a resident's car wears a body and a paint that are a pure pick of his number, whichever pool car stands for it. `carPoseOf(resident, out)` and `setCarDoor(resident, radians)` give the space pose and open the driver's door of the car that stands for him (`residentSpotAt` is the space a resident's car takes, pure in the layout). A resident's get-in or get-out clip is drawn only beside her car: one the traffic holds as hers at that space, or a kerb car brought to it when the space is empty and unseen (`OpenWorldTraffic.carForClip`, `KerbCars.bring`), held `CLIP_HOLD_SECONDS`. Otherwise the clip is not drawn.
- **Cost of cars** (`body-swaps.ts`, `engine/traffic-dress.ts`): a pool car is never rebuilt to change its body where an eye might see it: a resident's car takes a pool car that already wears his body (kerb cars evict an unseen anonymous car of that body first; leased movers prefer one), and only when there is none, the space is unseen and no player is near, and none was rebuilt in the last `BODY_SWAP_SECONDS` is one rebuilt; else his car stands in the body it has. A traffic car is dressed once when first stood on a space; after that only its paint (`TrafficDress`).
- **Evasive driving** (`TrafficDriver.think`'s `chaser`): a traffic car a patrol car chases (`evade`/`endEvade`, called by the law's `PatrolCars` each step of a chase) drives as `RaceBrain`'s evader does: gas on (`FLEE_SPEED`), no signal held, corners harder (`arcSpeed`), the line swung `LINE_W` + 0.9 m clear of the chaser's when it is close behind, on to far spaces until the chase ends.
- **Getting into another car on foot** (`engine-onfoot.ts`, `OpenWorldTraffic.isEmptyCar` / `takeCar`): on foot in the open world (host and solo) Enter at the driver door of any empty car (a kerb car, a parked mover, a car whose driver was thrown out, the car he left, a peer's that left) gets him in, with the same climb, range and speed rule as his own car; a car on a trip (a driver in it) is refused. The car leaves the pool for good (`humans`): traffic never drives, stores or paints it again, the resident whose car it was has none (`CARLESS`; his hidden drive leg still plays), and the car he left stays where it stands as an empty car.
- **Bodies for the civilians near a player** (`engine/openworld-bodies.ts`, `ragdoll-body-host.ts`): the civilians within 28 m of the eye (at most `BODY_COUNT`: desktop 6, phone 3, nearest first; walking, standing, talking, bumped, flinching or phoning; on the street, not on a stoop, not in a building's footprint) are physical walkers of the dummies' pool (`startWalker`, the player on foot's and the showcase figures'), steered to the crowd's spot for each, so cars, props and other bodies meet them as they meet any dummy; `CivilianArt` draws none of those (`held`). The crowd stays the authority on what happens to her: a car that runs her down (`takeStrikes`) knocks her live body in its slot (`knockWalker(strike, slot)`), never a separate throw that can start inside the car; a civilian with no body (far) is thrown as before. A body lying after a strike stays `DOWNED_LIFE_SECONDS`; one on her feet, steady and blocked more than 3 m from her spot is let go and she is drawn at her spot again. A body that falls or stumbles is kept: it lies, is asked to get up after 1.5 s, and runs back to her spot from where it stands (up to 15 m behind); still down after 8 s, or farther behind, it is let go. Each machine makes its own bodies from the crowd it has (no wire).
- **People in the road** (`TrafficDriver.people`, `OpenWorldTraffic.setPeople`): every car (urgent and fleeing ones too) looks along its route for a shown civilian within `PERSON_REACH` (2 m) of a route point and stops `PERSON_GAP` (4.5 m, centre to her) short of her, as for a car ahead; she is the crowd's drawn spot, so a body steered to it and the art draw her alike. The crowd does not wait at kerbs for the signal (the walk timing is OwSociety's), so a walker mid-crossing on a red still holds the car.
- **Ambulance queue** (`AmbulanceService.call(x, z, seconds, victim)`): a call while one is out waits its turn (`AMBULANCE_QUEUE`, 4; a fifth is refused) and goes when the ambulance has left; the medics kneel at the call it is out on (`ambulanceScene`). When loading ends `onAmbulanceLoaded(victim)` fires: the director takes that civilian's body away (her live body from the bodies, else the figure thrown for her, by the slot `throwFigure` gave and its age) instead of leaving her lying `DOWNED_LIFE_SECONDS`. A client keeps its own lying body (no wire).
- **Red-light runs** (`red-light-watch.ts`): `onRedLightRun(listener)` (host and solo) fires once per car per crossing of a stop bar against its signal, for every car, the player's included.
- **Engine** (`engine-openworld.ts`, `engine-race.ts`): `RaceDirector.freeDrivers` drives the lobby's cars no human does (null: they stand on their brakes as ever).

## Interiors (`world/openworld/interior-*`, `present/interiors`)

Five interiors (`Place.interior`: rowhome, coffee, bar, office, store) are built from one furniture kit. The **plan** (`planInterior`, pure, the same on every peer) lists walls with openings, floor and ceiling slabs, stairs and furniture in plan metres (`deep` from the street wall, `across` along the face), laid inside the building prop's drawn walls (`drawnHalf`: they stand short of the footprint, under the roof slab's overhang) a skin short of them on every side, so no interior wall covers the facade's picture; a place's door point is on the drawn front wall; the same plan gives
the **seats** (`Place.seats`: x, z, yaw, height; a rowhome's two stoop seats lead, `Place.stoopSeats`), the **counter** spots (`Place.counter`: where a customer stands to buy, where the clerk stands) and the **solids** (`interiorSolids`) the walkers' physics takes while an eye is near. A rowhome stands on its stoop's landing (0.9 m) with as many storeys as its lot has (below) and a flight between each pair up its left wall, the flights alternating between two feet so none stands over another's well, each walled off from the room beside it and opened through the floor above from the first tread a climber's head would meet it (`HEADROOM`, 2 m); a shop's floor is level with the sidewalk, and over it stand its flats (below).

The **doorways and shop windows are cut through the facade's ground band** (`SHOPFRONT` per interior; `scripts/pack-facades.mjs` zeroes the ground layer's albedo alpha there and the shader discards it), so the interior is seen from the street and a door leads into it. `InteriorSet` (one per map) keeps the
ground floor's envelope of every place within `SHELL_NEAR` of the eye, so no opening shows through to the world, and the furnished interior within `INTERIOR_NEAR`; both are dropped after the eye has been past their far radius for `INTERIOR_LINGER`. Within range an interior is built only while some eye sees it (the director's `SightField.seenThroughFace`: an eye inside it, or before its street face with a clear line to it, the view through the doorway and the windows; from behind or beside it is not seen) or a player stands within `INTERIOR_KEEP` of its footprint (inside, on the stoop, in the rear yard), and it is dropped once no eye has seen it for the field's hide grace. Its solids for the physics follow distance alone, and the seats and counter the crowd poses people at are map data the drawing never touches. Everything is packed into four meshes (envelopes, furniture, lamps and screens, glass; `present/interiors/interior-pack.ts`), the draw calls not growing with the interiors: a build writes its pieces after what is packed, in storage made once (doubled only when a build outgrows it, so it stops growing once the busiest street has been driven), a drop closes its range by moving what follows it down, and only the vertices that changed are sent to the card, so streaming rooms in and out while driving makes no geometry, buffer or array; a build's own work is bounded (`interior-art-alloc.test.ts`).

**Philadelphia.** From the owner's photographs of the real streets: a mid-block **lane** cuts through every block's south face but the park's and the garden's (lot 10, left bare and paved, a way from the alley to the street between two houses that then show their side walls: `LANE`, `layLane`), some **rear ells** (about two houses in five have a lower wing against their back wall, in the house's style, on the side a seeded mix picks; a solid `Building.annex` with no place, none behind a yard place), and two **landmarks** on the real facade pipeline, exterior only: a rough-stone church three lots wide (`church`: ground band of low arched windows, one tall storey of stained glass between buttresses, a corbelled cornice; a hangout with hours) and a modern four-storey infill two lots wide (`infill`: white panels, dark vertical window bays, a brick base; a home).

**Storeys.** Every lot has two to five storeys (`Place.storeys`, `world/openworld/openworld-storeys.ts`): a seeded walk west to east along each block face (mostly three, some four, a few two, now and then a run of low houses, the end lots a storey taller), so a street has a skyline. The building's height is `buildingHeight(storeys, shop)`, the facade's bands are laid for it (`faceBands(style, face, storeys)`: the ground band at the same height whatever the count, the storeys above it sharing what lies between it and the cornice, which keeps its depth), `buildingPieces(style, wall, slab, storeys)` draws a geometry for each count and the skin names it (`facadeSkin`: `rowbrick@4`; the usual three is the style's own name), and the interior's floors follow the same lines (`levelsOf(kind, storeys)`, 0.1 m over each storey line). A two-storey rowhome has no third floor; a five-storey one has four flights.

**Flats.** The floors over a shop are flats (a living room at the front, a kitchen along the left wall, a bed and a dresser at the back), reached by a service stair: a flight along one side wall at the back of the shop, walled in with a doorway near its foot (a shop keeps its fixtures clear of its strip), climbing toward the street and arriving in the first flat; the flights between flats alternate the right and left walls. A flat is dressed as a home (`lookKind`). The windows over a shop are the facade's, so a flat has an opening wherever the picture shows a window.

**Windows.** The facade's windows are the window table's (`world/openworld/facade-windows.ts`, its rects written by `scripts/pack-facades.mjs` from the masks that light them in `facade-windows.json`): `faceBands` is how `present/facade-mesh.ts` cuts every face of an open style (a plinth up to the interior's raised ground floor, then a storey band on each floor of the interior, so a ground window's sill is over the floor and not the kerb; side faces in as many bays as their depth holds), and `roomWindows` is what the interior cuts its walls from: an opening of the same size on the same face and storey for every window, a pane of glass over it (the fourth merged mesh), and, behind the rowhome's bay stack, an alcove (floor, apron, ceiling and three panes round `bayOutline`) the room opens onto. A house at a block's end (`Place.openSides`) shows its side wall to the cross street, windows on every storey, and its room has them. **See-through from both sides.** The packed albedo's alpha says what each texel is (`FACADE_ALPHA`): wall, doorway (cut for good) or window (cut while the room behind it is built, but for a door's transom); `InteriorSet` lists the buildings whose envelope and rooms above it are built (`setOpenFacades`, up to `FACADE_OPEN_MAX`, by the instance's x, z), the facade's vertex shader tells each instance whether it is listed (`vFacadeOpen`) and its fragment shader discards the window texels of a listed one, so from the street every window with an opening in the room behind it shows the lit room through the glass (the interior's translucent pane, tinted), on every place kind; a building whose rooms are not built keeps its windows painted (no cost: the picture is the window), and from inside the window opening shows the street through the same pane. A neon sign is the picture's, painted inside its window and cut with it; no sign stands in a room.

**Opening the windows.** A building whose rooms are built is listed in the facade's open list with how far open it is (`FACADE_OPEN_AT`: x, z, openness 0 to 1 a slot); the interior set raises its openness over `OPEN_FADE_SECONDS` once its envelope and furnishing are both built, and lowers it again before dropping them (a room is dropped only with its windows shut), so a facade never pops between painted and seen-through. The open style's program cuts a window texel of the albedo alpha's window class, and only where the glow strip says it is glass (a mullion or a frame inside the window's rect stays painted), by a screen-door dither against that openness: at full opening by day `OPEN_WINDOW_CUT` of the painted glass is cut, and the rest stays over the room with the picture's own tint and reflections; at night (`facadeGlow` 1) all of it is cut, so no stipple lies over the lit room. The kit's bay panes read the same list and cut at half open (`facadeOpenLoop` gives them the openness).

**Which storeys open, and how a room is lit.** Only a house (`roomsAbove` in `facade-layers.json`, written by the packer for the rowhome styles) opens the windows of every storey; a shop, bar, cafe or office opens its ground band alone, and its upper floors (the flats over it, which are built for the walker's sake) keep the facade's painted windows: nothing there is cut, so nothing shows through to a room nobody looks at. A bay window's alcove is closed at the wall: the wall's opening is as wide as the bay's outline and the glass is set in from it, so a return of the wall (`jambPieces`) fills the strip between the glass's end and the opening's edge, floor to head. A room is lit by its own lamps, screens and windows, baked per vertex when it is built (`interior-light.ts`: `lightingOf` once per plan, `bakeCorner` per corner): the `light` vertex attribute (lamp r, g, b and daylight) is written by `PackedMesh.add`, and the room material (a `MeshBasicMaterial`, unlit by the scene) multiplies each corner's colour by `light.rgb * mix(LAMP_ROOM_DAY, 1, facadeGlow) + light.a * DAY_SKY_GAIN * (1 - 0.92 * facadeGlow)`, so the shared `FACADE_GLOW` takes a room from its daylight by day to its lamps at night. Floors, ceilings and walls carry grids of `ROOM_CELL` cells so the falloff shows. A shut shop's lamp light is scaled to `CLOSED_GLOW` (`PackedMesh.scaleLamp`); its lamps and screens show `LAMP_DAY` of themselves by day. No work is added per frame.

**Doors.** Every doorway has a hinged leaf (`interiorDoors`; one instanced mesh for the whole map), shut until someone (a shown civilian or the player's walker) stands within `DOOR_REACH` of it, then swung into the room over `DOOR_SWING_SECONDS`; a swing is local drawing, with no collider and nothing on the wire. Behind each street door stands a small vestibule (one instanced mesh of inside-out boxes), drawn while the room behind it is not built. A home's room is built only while a player is inside it (`SightField.playerInside`) or one of its street doors is open and an eye within range sees that doorway (`seenThroughFace` narrowed to the doorway): a civilian swinging a door no player sees builds nothing. A business's room (shop, bar, cafe, office, store: every interior but the rowhome) is also built, with no door open, while an eye within `INTERIOR_NEAR` (kept to `INTERIOR_FAR` once furnished) sees its street front, the nearest `SHOPFRONT_ROOMS` of them (the facade's list of `FACADE_OPEN_MAX` buildings leaves the rest to homes, and holds the nearest built if more are), so a shop's windows show its lit room from the street; it is released as the others are, after the hide grace. The physics holds a built interior's solids within `SOLIDS_NEAR` of the eye, and a door's vestibule (floor, sides, back) while the player's walker is within `VESTIBULE_REACH` of it.

**Walking in.** A place with an interior stands in a drawn-only `buildingShell` (a `building` with no collider), and its ground floor's street, party and back walls are invisible-wall props (`interiorEnvelopeProps`, merged along each row of houses) that are whole but for the doorways, so a car is stopped and a walker passes a door. The rest (slabs, stairs, furniture) reaches the physics as fixed boxes, handed over by `InteriorSet.onSolids` while the eye is within `SOLIDS_NEAR`.

The middle block is dressed after Rittenhouse Square. Its three north-south paths, the east-west path and a round plaza at the block's middle are paved (cobble paint laid ahead of the lawn, so the grass takes only what the paving leaves). The plaza is one solid prefab (`plaza`) of bricks: a seat ring 0.45 m high and a back ring 0.85 m high round a paved circle, open where the four paths come in (3.2 m, a car passes), and a bronze dog on a stone plinth in one quarter; what is drawn is exactly what a car meets. Slat benches stand beside each path facing it every 12 m, a bin by every other, a lamp every 24 m on alternate sides, low hoop fences run behind the benches (each a 4 m section that is a knockable prop: a car closing at 3 m/s or less (`HOOP_FENCE.knockSpeed`) meets it as a solid, one faster breaks it loose to tumble like a cone, the prism's `knockSpeed`), and an octagonal pavilion stands on the south-west lawn. ## The day

The open world's light follows the crowd's clock: the game hour is the crowd's start hour plus its game minutes (`gameHourAtMinute`; a crowd second is a game minute, a game day 24 real minutes). `OpenWorldDirector.frameDay` tells the stage once a game minute (never a frame; `WorldStage.setDayHour`), and leaving the open world gives the time of day back (`setDayHour(null)`). No setting moves it: `setNight` is ignored while the cycle runs (H is the race view's key). The one key that does is the number pad's plus and minus (`SEAT_KEYS.dayForward` / `dayBack`), a dev/QA key to see the city at another hour, in solo only: `OpenWorldDirector.skipDay` skips the crowd's clock to the next whole game hour, or to the start of this one (the one before when it stands on a whole hour), and the light follows at once (one `setDayHour` call, so one sun hop; whole hours are the coarse step, a game day is 24 real minutes, an hour a real minute). The society cannot be run backwards, so minus is the hour before a day on: `CivilianCrowd.skipThrough` lives every tick between (some 120 an hour, 2760 for minus, one frame's hitch of milliseconds in the brain's step), a tick at a time as the sim's steps would, so a crime due in them is raised in the log and committed, and the weekday moves on with them. The skipped time is asked nothing of the cars, the cops or the player (no hit, danger or ambulance call is raised in it). In a room (host or client; the port `inRoom`) the clock is the room's and every peer's, no key moves it, and nothing is sent: `skipDay` is false. The HUD shows the game clock top left as a 12-hour time (`fmtClock12`), read in five-minute steps (`CLOCK_STEP_MINUTES`) so the HUD publishes rarely; a guest sees the host's.

`day-cycle.ts` turns the hour into a grade: `night` (0 by day to 1 at the dead of night; the stage scales it by its night depth, so full night is the night setting's look), `warm` (dawn and dusk turn the palette toward the sunset's), `lamps` (the street lamps and what burns at night come on as the day's share falls through 0.85 to 0.35), and the sun's height by the clock. The stage writes only uniforms and colours with it: the hemisphere, sun and fill, the fog and the sky's colour, the windows (`FACADE_GLOW`: each building lights at its own point of the dusk), the lamp heads and their pools, the smoke's and the environment's strength. The sky is the procedural dome, its stops, clouds, disc tint and stars blended between the day, sunset and night looks (`SkyDome.setCycle`): no picture is swapped. The baked environment (the PMREM of a time of day, `DaylightEnv`) is the day's all cycle long (`envLook`): only its strength follows the hour; a rebake is the one expensive thing a night would otherwise bring.

**Night windows by who is home.** A home's windows light only while someone is home and awake, not by a fixed chance. The society has no sleep state, it has rest: a resident who came home for rest dwells there to relieve the need, and the society's hour table (`HOUR_WEIGHTS`, the rest row) weighs rest 1 from ten at night to five, 0.1 by day. `society-awake.ts` counts, for every home each game minute (never a frame), the people there who have arrived and are awake: a resident resting at their own home is asleep once the hour's rest weight (drawn to the next hour's by the minutes) reaches their own bedtime weight, a number from 0.5 to 1 of their id, so the evening thins between eight and ten at night, the early riser is up from five and the night owl's dawn is seven; a guest, or anyone there for another purpose, is awake. The city has far more homes than residents (905 homes, at most 300 people), and the residents are out at venues all evening and come home late to sleep, so the simulated people alone leave the evening city dark. Every home therefore also has an imagined household (`society-households.ts`, `ImaginedHouseholds`), the people of the home nobody simulates: one to four people (25, 35, 20 and 20 per cent) drawn from the seed and the home's place, the same on every peer, each with a morning (up between five and half past seven, out one to two hours later), an evening (home between half past four and eight, three in ten out again for one to three hours from six to nine) and a bedtime drawn through a curve (a person is still up at ten with a chance of 55 per cent, at eleven 32, at midnight 14, at one 5, at two 2, and no one at three). On the real map about nine homes in ten have someone awake at eight in the evening, three in four at ten, one in three at midnight, one in ten at one, and none after three. A home with a simulated resident counts the resident (from the society, above) and its household together: the household stays home in the evening and lights the home on its own schedule while the resident is out, and the resident adds to it when home and awake, so the start street is lit at nine in the evening. `HomeLights` (`present/facade-homes.ts`) turns the count into a share of the home's windows (none, 35, 60, 75, 85 per cent for none to four or more awake) and moves each home's share a tenth a minute toward it, so a house wakes or goes to bed over a few seconds, its windows lighting and going out one by one at their own thresholds (the glow strip's green and the building's seed, as before); a light skip (the number pad's plus and minus) puts every share at the new hour's outright. The shares are one byte a home in a small grid texture (4 m cells keyed by the building's position, 32-bit on both sides; texel 0 holds no home) which the facade shaders of every style with doorways or windows cut in them read per building (`facadeHomes`), and so does the bay kit's window shader (`facade-bays.ts`, at the house's centre, not the frontage's); a building with no texel (a shop, a style that is no home) keeps the fixed share of 0.6, and a home with none awake gets exactly none. The texture is written and sent only for homes that moved. By day the glow is 0 and nothing is lit. It is drawing only, read off the crowd state each peer already has and the seed (no wire, no brain): every peer lights the same homes at the same hour.

The light's direction is the part a look pays for (the shadow camera's axes, `far`, the dome's sun): it moves only when the shadow tip of a three-storey rowhome would shift by `SUN_HOP_METRES` (`sunNeedsHop`; a low sun needs small steps, a high one none for a long while): about 300 hops a game day, one every couple of game minutes through the morning and afternoon, every minute in the golden hours, none at midday. Its path is the sun's arc (12 degrees at either end, 58.6 at noon) handing over to the moon's elevation as the night comes, in the shadow's length so the tip moves at a steady pace through the hand-over. A hop and a grade step cost under a microsecond of script each; the shadow map is redrawn every frame whether the light moved or not (nothing sets `shadowMap.autoUpdate`), so a hop adds no GPU work.

## Law: cops and criminals (`ai/openworld-crime`)

Foot cops patrol the sidewalks and patrol cars (leased from the traffic's pool) answer what the society holds against its residents: a crime, a fight, a red light run, a driver who keeps running people down.

- **What it reads.** `OffenceSource` (the brain's wanted spell, fight tick and partner, where a resident stands, when their walk began; `StateOffenceSource` reads the society's rows through `society-crime.ts`). A resident wanted and not yet taken up opens a **case**: a crime (one suspect) or a fight (two). At most `MAX_CASES` at once.
- **Foot law (`FootLaw`).** Runs on a fixed grid of crowd time (`CRIME_HZ`), a pure function of (seed, society rows, clock): `FootCops` walkers on the walk graph (the nearest free cops called, A\* runs by `PathFinder` from the nearest node the walker has a clear line to and to the nearest the aim has (`FootWalkers.nearestSeenNode`), the last `STRAIGHT_APPROACH` m straight only along a clear line (`straightClear`, the engine's `lineOfSight` over the buildings' footprints): nobody walks through a building, and a cop at a corner lot's front stoop does not cut across the lot to the side street), `Flight` for suspects. A suspect a cop has in sight (within `COP_SIGHT` and a clear sight line at `EYE_HEIGHT` past the course's buildings: the engine's `lineOfSight` port, `sightLine` over the course's hiding solids) runs, by streets weighted away from the case's cops, to the hiding place (`PLACE_KIND.hide` door) they reach `HIDE_LEAD` m ahead of the nearest cop, else to the street corner farthest from the cops, which they keep (`CaseTable.fleeNode`) until they reach it (`FLEE_REACHED`) or a cop is within `FLEE_KEEP_GAP` of it: a corner is never a door's spur nor nearer than `FLEE_MIN_LEG`. Hidden, they are searched for once when the first cop reaches the door (a seeded draw, `FIND_CHANCE`); the search is given up after `SEARCH_SECONDS` and they stay off the street until their next walk begins. A cop within `BUST_REACH` of a suspect takes hold; `GRIP_SECONDS` later the cuffs are on and she is taken to the police station (`Arrests`), where the host raises `EVENT_KIND.bust` (`CivilianCrowd.raiseEvent`; the society holds them in the station for jail, clears the wanted spell and both fighters' fight).
- **Beats.** A patrolling cop walks a beat, not a random walk: from corner to a corner (a node with a way on, never a door's spur) `PATROL_LEG_MIN` to `PATROL_LEG_MAX` m off, seeded (`CRIME_STREAM.patrolTurn`), not one of the last `PATROL_RECENT` he stood at, standing `PATROL_PAUSE_SECONDS` plus a seeded `PATROL_PAUSE_SPAN` at each; so a beat covers a block or two of ground and never loops a corner. A cop on a run keeps the route he has while it still ends at the aim's node (`FootCops.replanIfAimMoved`): planned afresh from the nearest node every half second, a slow walk (an escort) along a sidewalk past a door's spur would turn back into the spur and loop.
- **Arrests (`Arrests`).** The cop takes hold from `ARREST.cuffBehind` behind the suspect, both facing her way, for `GRIP_SECONDS` (the cuffing clip's length): she acts `cuffing`, he `arresting`. Cuffed, she is led at his right (`ARREST.escortAcross`, `escortAhead`) at `ESCORT_SPEED` (`cuffed` / `escorting`) to a patrol car (`PatrolCars.carForArrest`): the case's own car when it has come to its scene, else **his own** (below), left where it stands when within `HOME_CAR_NEAR` m and else called to the kerb nearest them. He leaves the sidewalk graph for the last `DOOR_APPROACH` m to the car's driver's door (`FootCops.leaveSidewalkFor`: the kerb space lies between), and within `BOARD_REACH` m of it, the car standing, they get in together (`SUBJECT_MODE.boarding`: the `carIn` clips at `PatrolCars.doorSpot`, she `BESIDE_COP` m to his side, the car's door swinging with the clip, `carDoorOpenness`); then they are off the street (`conveyed`, her cop `COP_MODE.riding`; the host logs it, `arrestRide`) and the car drives, siren off, along the streets to the kerb space nearest the station's door (`PatrolCars.convey`, at most `CONVEY_GIVE_UP_SECONDS`). There they get out (`alighting`, the `carOut` clips; logged as `arrestAlight`) and he walks her in at the station's door (`stationOf`: the door node of `Moore St Police Station`; `STATION_ESCORT_SECONDS` at most), where the host's bust (or jaywalk) event is raised: custody starts at the door and she is let out of it after the jail time and walks home (`goHomeFromCustody`). The case's other cops go back to patrol once every suspect is caught. His own car then drives back to park by his beat; a case's car is put away `CAR_LINGER_SECONDS` after, siren off. Where no car can be had (the pool has none free, none finds a way, or the car does not come in `CAR_BOARD_SECONDS`) her cop walks her all the way to the door; a client's law has no cars of its own, so it walks her too until the host's ride is heard (see "What a client sees of the host's scenes"). A cop run down lets her go. The player on foot's custody (below) runs through this too. A fighter thrown down is taken off the street at once and his event raised then. A map without a station has the cop lead her a few metres (`ESCORT_MIN_DISTANCE`, `ESCORT_SECONDS`) and the event is raised at the cuffs.
- **The cops' own cars.** Each foot cop has a patrol car of his own, `HOME_CARS` of the pool's `PATROL_CARS` (the cars past the `MAX_PATROL_CARS` a case or a chase calls; a pool with no spare gives none, as on a phone): `PatrolCars.keepHome` leases it at the first poll (again `HOME_RETRY_SECONDS` after a miss), siren off, to the kerb nearest his beat's start (`FootCops.homeNode`), where it stays parked (and does not see for the law) until an arrest by him calls it.
- **Fights.** A cop within `FIGHT_WARN_REACH` warns; after `WARN_SECONDS` each fighter's seeded draw (`RESIST_CHANCE`) says whether they keep attacking. The instigator is arrested whatever; a fighter who keeps attacking is taken down (a `FigureStrike` thrown as a figure, like a run-down civilian) and arrested; the other is let go with a warning. A fight that ends before a cop comes goes on as a pursuit of the fighters still wanted.
- **Seen, or told.** A wanted resident (a crime or a fight the society holds) opens a case only once the police know of it: a cop, on foot or in a patrol car, within `COP_SIGHT` with a clear sight line to them now, or a witness's call has ended. The call is read off the society's rows (`StateOffenceSource.reportDoneTick`): a resident within `WITNESS_RADIUS` of the crime place whose leg purpose is `PURPOSE.report` since it happened, ending when their dwell does (`REPORT_MINUTES`); the law notes it the first poll it sees it (so the witness going back to their day does not lose it). An offence nobody saw or reported brings no police until a cop comes into sight of the wanted resident.
- **The player on foot (`PlayerLaw`).** Host and solo player only (a client's walker is nobody's to judge here, and his cops are the host's to draw): crossing against the signal where a free cop sees him, or shoving a civilian where a cop sees it (the `walker` triple of the crowd's log, `OpenWorldLaw.hearShoves`), or a fight he started (`FIGHT_PLAYER`) the police learn of by sight or call, makes a chase of him: the foot cops run at wherever he is, and a cop within `FOOT_HOLD_REACH` while he moves slower than `FOOT_HOLD_SPEED` runs the bust hold as a car's (`SURVIVAL.bustTime`, resets when he moves off), shown by the same pursuit strip; full, the cop who holds him has him in custody (`PlayerLaw.arrest`; the strip is gone): his case's first subject slot (no resident: `CaseTable.isPlayerSlot`) is led exactly as a suspect is, by `Arrests` (the cop leads him at `ESCORT_SPEED` to his own patrol car, they get in, the car drives, siren off, to the kerb by the station's door, they get out; walked there on foot when no car can be had, which is always so on a phone, where the cops have no cars of their own, and a map with no station has the cop lead him a few metres), with no cuffing clip (his walker is the player's own ragdoll, not the crowd's art), his cop acting the `escorting`, `carIn` and `carOut` clips. The engine hears it through `FootArrestPorts` (`LawPorts.footArrest`, host and solo only): `busted`, `led` every grid step with where his cop leads him (his walker is driven there, `OnFootDirector.lead`), `taken` with the patrol car's place in the engine's list once they are in (`PatrolCars.carIndexOf`: his walker is gone and the camera follows the car), and `delivered` with where and which way he is set down: at the car's door at the station, or where he stood with his cop if a car runs the cop down on the way. See [`ON_FOOT`](ON_FOOT.md#under-arrest). He gets away when `PLAYER_CHASE_GIVE_UP_SECONDS` pass without the hold filling, or when he gets into a car. He has no drawing of the law's: his own walker is where he is. Nothing on the wire changes (the custody is the host's alone; `NET_VERSION` stays).
- **Jaywalking.** Twice a second every walker drawn out on a zebra crossing (`CrossingWatch`: off both kerbs, within `CROSSING_HALF_WIDTH`, heading along it) while the cars of the street they cross have green (`TrafficSignals` over the traffic lights' `lightState` at the nearest junction; every peer builds it from the layout) is stopped by the nearest free cop who sees them (`COP_SIGHT`, a clear sight line), once per walk. The cop walks over, takes hold, walks them to the police station, and the host raises `EVENT_KIND.jaywalk` there (the society's short custody, in the station); a walker who walks on out of the cop's reach for `JAYWALK_GIVE_UP_SECONDS`, or goes indoors, is let go.
- **Nobody a car hits is arrested.** A suspect (crime, fight or jaywalk) the society has in hospital (`FLAG.hurt`, a knock) is let go at once, and the case for that spell is not taken up again.
- **Cars (`PatrolCars`, `PursuitDriver`).** A foot case calls a patrol car to it (it stops `CAR_STOP_GAP` m short; its cop steps out and joins the case, `CAR_COP`); a red light run a cop saw (foot cop or patrol car within `RED_LIGHT_SIGHT` with a clear sight line) sends one after the car; a car that runs `HEAT_KNOCKS` civilians down within `HEAT_SECONDS` has one sent whoever saw it. Most runs drive like city police: a run to a scene is the traffic's urgent trip along the streets to the kerb space nearest it (lanes, turn speeds, slowing to a stop; `DrivenCar.arrived`), and a chase closes in braking to `CAREFUL_PURSUIT_SPEED` by the last `CLOSE_PURSUIT_DISTANCE` m. `HOTHEAD_RUN_SHARE` of runs (seeded per run from the host's seed) are a hothead's, straight at the target at full speed, so a cop wrecking himself on a tree stays possible, not every time. A careful chase is the same trip, re-aimed every `REDIRECT_SECONDS` (`OpenWorldTraffic.redirect`) at the kerb space nearest where the offender will be in `CHASE_LEAD_SECONDS`; parked within bust range of the offender it waits (the hold runs); stopped short of one that left the road (more than `BUST.near`, within `CLOSE_PURSUIT_DISTANCE`) the police driver takes the wheel for the last metres, at `CAREFUL_PURSUIT_SPEED`, and gives it back past `CHASE_RESUME_DISTANCE`. Every patrol driver brakes for a car standing or crawling on its line (`CAR_AVOID_*`). The hothead's (and the close-in chase's) driving is the police's: `pursuitSteer`, `arcSpeed`/`brakeOver` over `Pedals`, the hunters' heading search over the course's solids (`Obstacles`) and `Backoff`. A traffic car it comes within `YIELD_DISTANCE` of is made to stop (the traffic hands its driver slot over); the bust is the race's rule, `BUST` (held under `BUST.kph` within `BUST.near` m for `BUST.time` s), and Survival's `SURVIVAL.bustTime` for a human's car, whose bust the engine's `playerBusted` port answers.
- **Foot cops are people in the street.** Their poses (and a suspect's) are drawn blended between the law's last two grid steps (`FootWalkers.snapshot`, `drawnX`/`drawnZ`/`drawnYaw`/`drawnStride`, at `CrimeScene.drawShare`), so they move as smoothly as the crowd at any frame rate; their stride is the civilians' per metre at a walk and never quicker than `RUN_STRIDES_PER_SECOND` at a run (`FootWalkers.carry`). Before each crowd step every cop on the street is a body the civilians step round, one that gives no way and shoves nobody (`OpenWorldLaw.feedCops` into `CrowdWalkers.addBody`). On the host a car runs a drawn cop down by the crowd's own test (`runsDown` within `CAR_REACH`): he is thrown in uniform, is off the street for `COP_DOWN_SECONDS` (out of his case; a suspect in his hands walks free), then back at his beat's start; the host raises `EVENT_KIND.copDown` and a client puts him down when its clock reaches it, read from the crowd's log.
- **Drawing.** The suspect's pose in `CrowdPoses` is overwritten inside the crowd's step (`CivilianCrowd.overlay`, after the civilians are placed and before the cars' hits, so a car meets a fleeing suspect where they are drawn; `overlaySuspects`); the foot cops are a second crowd (`copPoses`, look word `POLICE_HASH`) drawn by their own `CivilianArt`.
- **The bust strip.** While patrol cars chase the player's car the HUD shows Survival's own pursuit strip (`PursuitStrip`, the same look and places: bottom centre, or the top column on a touch screen) with the same fill and drain: 'Chased by N cops', and once a patrol car has the car held under `BUST.kph` within `BUST.near` m, the bust hold running out over `SURVIVAL.bustTime` s (`PatrolCars.chaseOf`, the race's rule: it resets the moment the car drives off). `OpenWorldDirector.bustOf(carIndex)` hands the HUD one object while the figures read the same (`CrashHudState.bust`); the strip is gone when the chase ends (the bust, the car got away). HUD only: nothing on the wire.
- **Netplay.** Every peer runs the foot law; only the host raises events, and a suspect's bust is the host's event heard back through the log. A peer that joins late starts its chases from the open wanted spells it finds, so its cops' positions are its own until the host's bust ends the case. Patrol cars are host sim cars and stream like any car.
- **Wiring.** `engine/engine-openworld-law.ts` (`OpenWorldLaw`) is made with the crowd and driven from `OpenWorldDirector.step/frame`; the director makes the law after the traffic and gives it the traffic (`OpenWorldTraffic` satisfies `TrafficApi`); `ports.law` (engine) supplies the course's solids (`RaceField.courseObstacles`), the light bars, the bust of a human's car, which takes it back to its spawn (`resetLobbyCar`: the lobby has no session, so Survival's banner and chase strip have nothing to show), and `footArrest`, the player on foot's arrest (`OnFootDirector.arrest`, `lead`, `take`, `deliver`). Without `ports.law` there are no police.
