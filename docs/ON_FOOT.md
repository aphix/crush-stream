# On foot

The player gets out of the car, walks and runs as the crash-test dummy, is knocked down by a car, gets up, and gets back in. The
walker is an active ragdoll: the same twelve Rapier boxes and eleven joints as a thrown driver (`present/ragdoll-body.ts`: feet on ankle hinges, 20° toes up and 30° down), held up by
joint motors, not a second animated mesh. The same walker, one per pool slot, stands the showcase's figures up again after a press
knocks them over ([The showcase](#the-showcase)).

## Controls

| Action | Keyboard and mouse | Controller |
|---|---|---|
| Get out of your car (driving, stopped or under 3 m/s, driver door clear); get back in (on foot, at your own car's driver door) | Enter | Y / Triangle |
| Walk, relative to the camera (stick: slower for a lighter push, 0.8 m/s to a run, past the pad reader's one 0.15 dead zone and curve 1.7, `gamepad.ts`) | W A S D, the arrows | left stick |
| Run (a key is a full push, the stick walks up to it) | W A S D, the arrows | left stick pushed all the way |
| Sprint (7 m/s) | Shift | A / Cross, or RT past halfway |
| Hold to a walk (1.5 m/s) | Space | X / Square |
| Look round (the same orbit as everywhere) | mouse drag, `;` mouse look, scroll | right stick |
| Get up after a fall | any walking control | any walking control |

Nothing on foot is a new control: the keys, sticks and buttons that steer, gas, boost and handbrake the car walk, run, sprint and hold to a walk
(`engine-walk-input.ts` reads the same `DRIVE_KEYS` and `PadState`; its rows pair every walking control with its driving twin).
Enter and Y were free in the driving seat (`SEAT_KEYS.onFoot` in `engine-keys.ts`; `engine-keys.test.ts` keeps it clear of the scene keys).
While he is out, the seat's own keys (Space, R, Esc, Q, E, V, C, T and the pad's Back, LB, RB, B and D-pad down) do nothing, so a stray press
cannot pause, reset or switch the car under him; Escape and Back still end mouse look and a menu as everywhere.

## Where it is offered, and why

Everywhere the player drives a car of his own, offline or online: the Fleet, a race (single, campaign, Survival, a room's), the derby, the Range's mini
games and the staged sandbox scenes (the press, pistons, doors, the stack, the corkscrew). Not in the garage, the lab or the showcase (no car of his to
leave), and not while a reel or clip is on screen, the game is paused or warming up, a scene is fading, or a race's menu is up (`CrashEngine.onFootOffered`,
the `available` of `OnFootHost`). He gets back into his own car only: the one he got out of. Another car's door is no door of his.

**What the rules see.** Nothing changes for them. A player on foot is a driver with his foot hard on the brake: the car he left stands on its brakes
(`brake: 1`, the `hold` input a racer waiting on a grid gets) and every rule reads that, not an empty seat.

- Outside the open world the walker writes nothing to a car. Not `driverOut` (that is a thrown-out driver: it rides the netplay snapshot and the highlight keyframes, makes
  the race's judge count the car a wreck and the HUD say DRIVER OUT, and the ejection watch skips the car), not `parked` (every drive call clears it), not
  the driver's door (an unlatched door at an angle is sim: the crash model hits it, and the clips record its hinge). The seated driver is left out of
  the draw (`SeatedDrivers.sync`'s `away`). In the open world, on a machine that runs the cars, the driver's door swings open as he gets out or in and shuts
  behind him (once seated, or once he is 4 m away): it is the car's real door, set as a resident's is. In every other scene the door stays shut (the walker
  writes nothing to a car).
- `CrashEngine.fixedStep` holds the car the walker left (`onFoot.away`) with the brake input outside a race, and takes it out of the derby AI's and the
  wreck coast's loops, as it does the car the player drives. In a race the director holds it itself (`RaceHost.afoot`, `RaceDirector.drive`), and the
  rules count it a human's car: `judge` does not call it dead for standing still, `aiDrives` is false so the stall check never asks for its respawn, and
  the race AI never drives it. Its row stays racing, with every other rule as for any car at the wheel (a reset the player asks for, a bust, the finish).
- A derby's rules are the derby's: the player's car is alive while its drivetrain is. If his car is the last one alive he wins, on foot or not (the
  count-out clocks run on the car as they do on one at the wheel).
- The ejection watch judges the car as it always did; a driver it would throw is not there, so the dummy is not launched (`throwDriver` and the netplay
  `launchEjection` skip the car he left), while the recorder and the wire hear the event as before.
- A race's pause (Esc, Start) and a minigame's own keys stay theirs; R and the other seat keys do nothing on foot, as ever.

**Netplay.** The walker is local presentation: a peer sees the car, parked on its brakes on the host and coasting there while a client's input is idle,
and never a walker. The walker itself rides no message, no snapshot or keyframe carries a dummy or a door, and the race recorder and the
reel keep recording the car's inputs as today. The dummies' world is per browser; `ragdolls.update`'s `authority` still gates only the glass. The law's arrest of him on foot (below) is the host's decision for every player on foot, its own or a guest's: the host's chase and arrest of a player reach every peer as events of the crowd's log, and the arrested player's own machine leads him through custody.

A scene reset (the HUD's reset, a scene change, a race starting, a loop reset) empties the dummy world and puts him back in the seat; the loop's own
reset waits while he is out. A race that takes his car from him (he is put to spectating, his car is put away) ends the trip where it stands.

**His world.** He lives in this browser's dummies' Rapier world, never the worker's: starting him begins the run again there (a worker holding the
run's thrown drivers would not step him). A track's ground reaches 48 m round the patch built for him, so the patch is built again round him once
he is 24 m from its middle (`RagdollThrower.followWalkerGround`); a bounded scene (the derby, the Fleet disc) builds its fixed colliders for the
whole scene at his first step.

## The state machine

`present/onfoot-flow.ts` (rows in `onfoot-flow.test.ts`):

```
inCar --press, stopped, door clear--> exiting --0.8 s--> onFoot --press at a driver door--> entering --0.6 s--> inCar
```

Refusals, each with its prompt: the car is moving over 3 m/s (`Stop the car first`), another car, a lamp post or the edge of the ground (no ground a
metre round the spot) is within reach of the door, or the car is on its side or roof (`No room to open the door`; in the open world a front seat whose
own door has no room is left by the far front door when the seat beside it is empty, as cars parked side by side in a room need: `exitSide`,
`OpenWorldSeats.exitSideOf`, the one rule for his own machine's prompt, his climb and the host's door swing; a guest's get-out act names the door
his machine chose, and the host swings that door or refuses him when it has no room there, so he never climbs out through a door that stays shut;
a guest whose own machine finds no door with room is refused there and sends nothing), his own car's driver door is not
within 1.9 m (`Walk up to your car's driver door`); a press while exiting or entering is ignored. `engine/engine-onfoot.ts` carries out what the flow
decides: on exit the dummy is placed at the door in the player's own look and facing the car's way and the seat is stepped back (the car is the engine's
to hold, above); on entry he turns to the car, and after the climb the dummy leaves the pool and the seat takes the wheel (`seat.mode = "drive"`).

### Under arrest

In the open world the police can arrest any player on foot, the host's own or a guest's, by the same rules ([`OPEN_WORLD`](OPEN_WORLD.md): `PlayerLaw`, one for each player, by his car index), and the flow has a fifth phase, `arrested`, entered from any phase he is out in (`OnFootFlow.arrest`) and left only when the law sets him down (`OnFootFlow.released`, to `onFoot`). The host decides the arrest and logs it; the machine whose player it is runs this flow from its own law, driven by the host's events and the cop as that machine draws him:

```
onFoot --law: busted--> arrested --law: taken (patrol car)--> arrested, walker gone --law: delivered (x, z, yaw)--> onFoot
```

`OnFootDirector` carries out the law's four calls (`FootArrestPorts`, wired in `engine.ts`). `arrest`: the phase changes and nothing he presses acts (the press is refused as busy; the seat keys, the reset and the driving keys are swallowed as on foot, so R is not the old instant reset into a car); the prompt reads `Under arrest`. `lead(x, z)`, every law step: his walker walks (`WALK_SPEED`) to where his cop leads him, whatever he presses. `take(carIndex)`: the patrol car has him aboard, so his walker leaves the pool, the orbit's trip view ends and `seat.focus(carIndex)` puts the camera on the patrol car. `deliver(x, z, yaw)`: his own car, repaired, is put on the road nearest the spot (`RaceField.placeNear`, the reset's own placement; its spawn where none is clear), his walker is started at the spot facing `yaw` (the way `beginExit` starts one), the seat follows his car again and the phase is `onFoot`: the controls are his, and the car is a door he can walk to. The car he left stays held where he left it until then (`away`). A scene reset ends the trip as it always does; the law's late calls then find no `arrested` phase and do nothing.

## The walker: a driven root and a powered ragdoll

`present/ragdoll-walker.ts` holds one `Walker` per pool slot; the player's is slot 3. Friction cannot give a body 12 m/s² (a run in 0.3 s) or 7 m/s, and the old open-loop
gait reached 1.3 / 2.5 m/s, so his root is driven the way a character controller drives a capsule, and the ragdoll is posed and powered to follow it
(onfoot-active-ragdoll, onfoot-rapier-ragdoll research):

- **The root** (`walker-locomotion.ts`). Speed along his facing closes on its target with its acceleration capped (12 m/s² to a run, 3.5 m/s: 0.3 s, and 4.5 m/s² on to a sprint, 7 m/s: about a second; 14 / 10 m/s² braking: a run stops in
  0.28 s and 0.5 m, a sprint in 0.6 s and 2.1 m) and the acceleration itself capped (`JERK` 175 m/s³), so a start and a stop ease in over about 0.07 s and the lean, cadence and foot reach read a smooth acceleration, not a square wave (`APPROACH` 30 /s closes the last of the gap; under `STOP_SNAP` with nothing asked he is stopped). The facing turns at `10 m/s² / speed`, at most 8 rad/s, so a sprint curves wide and a walk turns on the spot, and he leans with his speed and
  acceleration and banks into a turn (onfoot-direction-change research): the target speed is one continuous curve of the heading error with no bands: a heading under 30° off curves; 30-75° costs up to 30 % of the speed; past 75° it falls to a pivot's 2 m/s; past 120° to a stop and a turn. A sprint turned 45° faces the new way in 0.42 s keeping 6.5 m/s; turned 180° it faces the new way in 0.92 s, slowing to 1.4 m/s, and is back at speed 0.38 s after facing. The keys' direction eases to a new one over 0.08 s (`INTENT_SECONDS`), so a corner is turned, not snapped.
- **The frame holds him.** A kinematic frame follows the gait's torso centre; the torso hangs off it by a joint whose three angular motors (60 kN·m/rad on his feet, 3 kN·m/rad in the get-up) and three linear motors
  (150 kN/m: a planted foot is locked, so the hip must be where the gait put it) hold it, with his weight and the root's push fed forward as a force on the torso. A torso held back more than 0.2 m (a wall) brings the root back to it and caps his speed there.
- **The gait** (`walker-gait.ts`, `walker-leg.ts`). Feet are set down in the world and stay there (a planted foot's target moves by under 1e-9 m) while the pelvis rides over them, then swing to where it will pass over the
  next spot; a stance share of 57.5 % at a walk, 38 % at a run and 22 % at a sprint gives a walk's double support (15 %) and a run's flight. The pelvis stands as high as its planted legs allow (the drawn torso bobs 2.4 cm at a walk, 4.4 at
  a run), each leg is a two-bone solve to its foot (hip swing, hip roll, knee; the knee's target follows the solve at 30/s, since near a straight leg a few mm of reach swing it tens of degrees), the arms swing fore-aft about the vertical against the legs (the opposite thigh's swing about its own mean, resting back by 0.6 of the lean) with the elbows
  held near a right angle running and never closing past 100°, so a hand travels from hip to chest height and never above the shoulder; the pelvis turns against the hips, the head levels against the lean (0.9 of it,
  its neck motors a little soft so it lags), and the motors lead their targets by the target's own rate. The gait reads the torso's actual orientation, not the commanded one, so the legs follow what the torso really does.
  Lean 4° at a walk, 8° at a run, 15° at a sprint plus the push of an acceleration, and a bank up to 26° in a cut. Cadence 126 / 180 / 300 steps a minute (research 100-130 walking, 150-210+ running; the sprint steps fast to keep its swing in the running range; under a walk it falls to 72 a minute at a stand, so a creep takes short slow steps, not a shuffle), steps ahead per speed, and braking steps reach out (natural-gait research).
  The feet roll (`walker-rocker.ts`): heel strike, flat, then the heel rises onto the toe, so a stance rolls over the foot instead of the hip swinging the whole stride. Hip swing per cycle (peak to peak, walk / run / sprint) 41 / 65 / 62° (research 30-45 walking, 50-70 running).
  The drawn hands keep the mesh's own shape (no finger bones to curl). A swing foot within 3 cm of the ground carries the pelvis (`allowedHeight`) only while it is within 0.5 m of the hip: a foot reaching further for a landing it has not made (the turn of a run reversed) would fold the planted leg under him to keep the pelvis at its reach.
  **Coming to rest.** Under 0.15 m/s and 0.4 rad/s he is still: the cycle no longer lifts a foot on its clock, only a foot more than 5 cm from where a standing foot goes (beside the pelvis) lifts, one at a time, and swings home over 0.4 s; the soles lie flat (a foot that had rolled onto its toe turns on its heel again), and once both are home and planted the cycle is parked. A walk stops standing in about 0.2 s, a run in 0.6 s. (Before, the clock kept lifting a foot every half cycle and the two were never home and on their heels at once, so after any walk, even a 0.2 s tap, he stepped in place for good; and a walker seeded again kept the last trip's toe pivot and pitch.)
- **The feet** are 0.25 m boxes from heel to toe, the heel 0.03 m behind the ankle; the shins end at the ankle. A foot is 5 kg, 2.5 times water's density (a foot of water under a shin four times its mass was pressed 5-6 cm into the ground at the Range's landings, and lighter feet left a throw at the high jump's net sitting up against it), and the shin is lighter by as much, so shin and foot together weigh the footless shin's 9.45 kg: a throw carries the mass it was tuned on. The skin and a shoe over the ankle blend shin to foot alike (`ankleShare`), so no ankle bend pulls one through the other.
- **Planted feet are locked** (`walker-feet.ts`). A foot the gait has set down is held by its sole's pivot (the heel, then the toe as the foot rolls) to a kinematic anchor with three linear motors (100 kN/m, 2.8 kN·s/m) that come on over 2 frames after the touchdown and let go the moment the gait lifts it, in every state but standing (the get-up and a fall free both).
  A touchdown always takes the foot's own spot, whatever the frame: a frame as long as the ramp (34 ms, under 29 fps) grips fully at once, and the lock then held the spot of the foot's last touchdown, the world's origin for a walker's first, so every walker pulled his feet to (0, 0) at 100 kN/m and was flung at 40-130 m/s (the showcase's dummies on a slow device; `ragdoll-walker-slow-frames.test.ts`, `showcase-slow-frames.test.ts`).
  The motors, not friction, keep it still: before the lock the leg's own motors chasing the gait made the footless shin slide 0.22 / 0.47 / 1.06 m/s at a walk, run and sprint; the rows hold the sole's pivot under 0.1 / 0.1 / 0.2 m/s on average (`ragdoll-walker-pace.test.ts`). The lock is softer than the torso's hold (150 kN/m) on purpose: at 1 MN/m it took up every mismatch between the gait and the legs in the torso, which sagged 5-8 cm off its root, and the hit read below took that for a blow (3.8 of the 4 m/s that knock him down in every stand-up turn: 10 of 64 pushed-onto-his-back runs and 8 of 48 run-and-reverse runs fell, against 0 of 16 pushes before the feet).
- **Steps** (`walker-floor.ts`; the foot's rise in `walker-gait.ts`). Toward a step he slows no faster than 85 % of his brake allows (`PACE_BRAKE_SHARE`, so a run or a sprint reaches the first riser at the step pace and plants on the first tread), a sprint ramps as a run within 0.6 m of one, and a foot over a streamed box two treads or more over him (a wall), or across one from his root (a banister's far side: `WalkFloor.walled`), reads the floor he stands on rather than the ground under or beyond the wall. His floor is the active ground asked from his planted feet's height: a prism top (a stoop's tread, a curb) at most 0.30 m over them is floor, taller is a wall that his
  dummy's colliders stop him at (the prisms are the very solids `courseSolids` builds), and the course's solids (a low planter or a landing, read from the armed solids) count too, as do flights of stairs (`world/stairs.ts`: a solid box prop placed with `stairs: true`, whose top falls from its height at its local -x end to the ground at its +x end in 0.28 m treads): over one, the tread is his floor (the flat box it is placed as is not read), and the dummies' world stands a box per tread in place of the block, for a block as high as the top of the flight would be a wall to his feet. Boxes a building streams in count the same: `RagdollSystem.setFixedBoxes(boxes)` (whole list each call, `[]` to stream out) makes them fixed cuboids in the dummies' world, in the worker's too (a `boxes` message, one transferred Float64Array), and every walker's `floor.boxes`. A dummy asleep on a box that goes is woken so it falls. A foot rests on the higher floor under its heel
  and its toe, and a swinging one rises onto a step over a 1.2 slope beyond its toe, not at the edge. A step or a drop of 0.11 m or more within 0.6 m ahead of him holds his pace to 0.5 m/s (the gait's stride is then 0.24 m, a tread), because at a walk a stride is 0.7 m, three
  treads, and its landing would lie inside a riser or in the air. So does a step under his root itself: mid-flight his root lags the foot a tread up that sets his floor, and read only from 0.2 m ahead the next riser looked a stride away, so he sped up, planted a tread too high and folded (which foot reached a riser first decided it, so a walk pressed into one side of a flight fell where its mirror image climbed). Held back by the edge of a wall ahead at his chest that a sidestep of at most 0.25 m clears (a newel post, a door jamb: it cuts the line across his shoulders 0.2 m ahead of him and not the line through him, `WalkFloor.edgeAhead`), he sidesteps it at 1 m/s (`SIDESTEP_SPEED`) instead of pressing his shoulder on it. A ramp's gentler rise leaves his pace alone.
- **Hits.** One knock rule. (1) What his own motion explains never weakens or drops him: his torso's lag behind the root that holds it, as far as the root has travelled, is forgiven (`Walker.slack`), a wall ahead of him does not count (it only stops him), nor does any solid that only stops his own motion (a wall brushed at an angle, a door frame, a banister, a stair riser: a change of the torso's velocity inside the ball whose diameter is the root's velocity reversed), nor his torso springing up past the root in his own hop over something his foot caught (`hopUp`; up to 3 m/s, `HOP_EXPLAINS`: faster is read as a blow), nor his torso going out along the way `getOut` carries him out of something he was caught in (`carried`; up to 3 m/s, `CARRY_EXPLAINS`), and at full strength his balance does not give up because he is tipped; the root stops 8 cm (`LEASH`) after a wall stops his body. (2) A body meeting a body is judged by the showcase's law `knocksDown` (`RagdollThrower.meetWalkers`): closing at 2 m/s or more, the one run into goes down and the runner staggers; slower, both keep their feet. (3) Beside another body a lean can stagger him but not fell him (`Walker.beside`). (4) Anything else that reaches `feelHits` (a strike nobody modelled) is read against the old bar: his torso moving more than 1 m/s differently from the root takes strength in proportion (the motors weaken and he stumbles), past 4 m/s he is knocked down. The ball of (1) is read for every blow, so the bar for one across his way rises with his speed: at v m/s a sideways change d reads √(d² + v²/4) − v/2, and he goes down past √(16 + 4v) m/s (4 standing, 4.7 at a walk, 5.5 at a run, 6.6 at a sprint). The stiff hold takes a blow's speed out of the torso within a frame, so the read is the torso's speed relative to the root plus the hold's spring rate (√(150 kN/m / 100 kg) = 39/s) times how much of its displacement his own travel does not explain. Past a 60° tilt or under 0.75 m over the floor under his feet he has fallen and goes limp (8 % of the limb stiffness, no balance). A car at 4 m/s or more that meets him (`runsDown`) throws him with the lawn
  guards' and the VIP's launch (`fell`, in `scenes/figure-strike.ts`): the car's velocity with a lift, a kick to the side of its centre line and a tumble, ignoring that car for 0.35 s.
- **Getting up.** Unchanged: once he has lain still for a second and the player presses a walking control, he funnels into one of three routes by how his torso lies (`walker-getup.ts`): *feet down* regrounds; *face down*
  pushes up to hands and knees, kneels, rises; *face up* rolls onto his stomach first. Laid on his side, his feet tip him onto his stomach as he settles, so he takes *face down*. Stages are poses, a frame lean and a height, the limb motors ramp from limp over half a second, and a bounded
  lift carries the torso. It takes 2.3 s from the stomach and 3 s from the back after the second's lie, and then the root is seeded where he stands (`enterStand`): he runs at 3.15 m/s within 0.6 s of standing.
- **Cost.** Rapier steps 1/480 s inside the ragdoll system's own world (inline, never the worker's, in every scene, so the pad and keys reach the motors the frame they are read). Measured on the node probe,
  60 Hz frames: 0.034 ms a Rapier step and 0.28 ms a frame with the walker out, against 0.039 and 0.34 ms before; the gait and root cost under 0.01 ms a frame. A car near him is a kinematic proxy of its cage there, so a
  car that runs him down is the same one the sim moves (`ragdoll-walker-car.test.ts` drives a car through him and holds its sim state bit for bit against the same drive without him).

## Fists

On foot in the open world, solo, as a room's host or as a guest, fire throws a punch (`OnFootArms`, `engine-onfoot-arms.ts`: left click or RT; right button held or LT aims, the camera drawn in over his shoulder and him facing where it looks). A punch reaches 1.4 m in a 120-degree arc about his facing (`punchGap`), one every 0.6 s, and lands 0.18 s after the press (`OpenWorldActs.punchBy` for the actor, then `OpenWorldDirector.punch`). It staggers a civilian or a cop; the third on one civilian inside 10 s knocks her out, and the third on one cop puts him down. A punch on a civilian is a shove to the law; a knock-out, or a punch on a cop, is a fight the law knows of at once. Where no civilian or cop is in reach, another player on foot in reach takes it (`PlayerBlows`: staggered, and knocked down by his third inside 10 s, as a civilian is); where nobody is, a knockable prop is knocked. A civilian's swings stagger him, and the third inside 4 s puts him down. A guest swings at once and sends the punch to the host as an act (`MSG.act`, [`MULTIPLAYER.md`](MULTIPLAYER.md#acts-of-a-guest-on-foot)); the host carries it out from the guest's own place, so what it did comes back to every peer the way the host's own punch does (the crowd's event log).

## Guns

The number keys choose the weapon in hand on foot in the open world (`OnFootArms.weapon`; LB / RB step through them): 1 fists, 2 pistol, 3 shotgun, 4 rifle (`WEAPONS`, `engine-onfoot-arms.ts`). A shot is a ray to the first thing it meets and a speed added there, as a click on a showcase dummy is, sized by the gun:

|Gun|Speed a hit adds (m/s)|Range (m)|Rays a shot|Scatter (rad)|Between shots (s)|Held fire repeats|Push on a car (N·s)|Prop knocked at (m/s)|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|Pistol|6|120|1|0.005|0.25|no|150|5|
|Shotgun|3.5|40|8|0.04|0.9|no|37.5|8|
|Rifle|9|250|1|0.0013|0.12|yes|250|7|

With a gun or the hook in hand his right arm is held out in front of him (`Walker.holdGun`: the thrown punch's shoulder and elbow targets, held) and the weapon lies in his hand, its grip at the hand and its barrel at what his aim is on (`HandGuns.pose`, `engine-hand-gun.ts`, scalar math and its mesh's matrix written in place, so a frame allocates nothing). Each weapon has its own model (`present/weapon-models.ts`, `gunModel`: a pistol 0.22 m long, a pump shotgun 1.04 m, a rifle 0.98 m and the hook's launcher 0.37 m, each built in code from slabs traced off its side view, a colour per vertex, under 400 triangles, with its grip point and its muzzle's place along the barrel and above the grip); the one mesh in his hand wears the model of the weapon in hand (`HandGuns.model`, the one place a weapon becomes a mesh), and the lawn guards hold the pistol (`People`). The bore is above the grip, so the barrel is tipped down by the angle that puts the target on the bore's own line (`HandGuns.toward`). The aim ray, through the view's centre dot (mouse captured, aim held, his own eyes) or the cursor, only finds the target: the nearest of a knockable prop, a ragdoll (a pool slot: another player's body too), a drawn civilian, a foot cop or a car along it, within the gun's range, unless a wall or the ground is nearer, looked for from the ray's point nearest his torso onward (`OpenWorldDirector.meet`, which strikes nothing), else its point at the gun's range. At the moment of the shot the gun is laid from his hand with its bore on that target (`HandGuns.layOn`) and held there for the muzzle flash (`FLASH_S`), then eased back, so the barrel, the tip and the bullet are one line; the bullet leaves the tip and goes to the target (`OpenWorldActs.aimFromMuzzle`), as a shot converges on where the camera looks: a car or a dummy between his muzzle and a target the camera sees over takes the shot. When the target is nearer his torso than the tip is, or a wall of a place stands between his torso and the tip (he is pressed against it), the ray starts at his torso instead. Firing at a target behind him, more than a quarter turn off his facing, turns him to it (`OnFootDirector.faceToward`) and holds him from walking while he turns; a target to his side or ahead does not stop his run. The nearest thing along the shot's ray takes it (`OpenWorldDirector.shoot`). A person is knocked down along the shot (a knock, as a car's). A car is pushed along it; a glass pane cracks, then shatters; its body sparks, and its engine dies after 13 to 19 hits on the body (the lawn guards' count). Every shot opens a police chase of the player who fired it, seen or not. There is no ammunition. The shot's rays are scattered on the host (`OpenWorldActs.shootBy`) from a seeded stream of each actor, the host's own player's included, so no act can be replayed for a better spread. A guest sees its own flash, tracer and sound at once and sends the shot to the host as an act whose eye is his gun's tip and whose direction is toward the target; the host fires it for that actor and refuses it when the line passes farther than `SHOT_RAY_REACH` (2.9 m: the tip's 0.8 m plus the most the host's view of a sprinting guest lags across a shot to his side, 7 m/s x 0.25 s = 1.75 m, plus 0.3 m of margin; a room's interpolation delay, foot-message interval and latency make that view 0.22 to 0.32 s old) from his torso, when the line's start is farther than `SHOT_ORIGIN_REACH` (4 m: the shotgun's tip 1.4 m from his torso, 2.24 m of lag at the oldest view, 0.3 m of margin) from it, or when a wall stands between his torso and that start. Each act spends the gun's whole cooldown, with a 0.5 s backlog (`ACT_BACKLOG_S`) for bunched delivery, so the long-run rate is the gun's.

A building's walls stop a shot from both sides (`placeMet` asked as a bullet asks, `shot`): a ray that starts inside a building's footprint meets the wall it would leave by, so nobody shoots out of a shop through its walls, and the wall test between two points (`OpenWorldDirector.wallBetween`) finds a wall between them unless both lie in the same building or the line runs through a street doorway. The openings are the street doorways the facade and the room are cut with (`SHOPFRONT`, one set a frontage bay: a rowhome's and a coffee shop's 1 m wide, an office's 1.5 m, a store's 0.9 m, 2.1 to 2.4 m high from the sill; a rowhome's sill is its stoop's landing): a shot passes through one out into the street, or in from the street to the wall behind. The look panel's ray is not a bullet: it still passes out of the building its eye is in, as through a window.

## The hook

`0` takes the grappling hook in hand, weapon 0 of `OnFootArms` (LB / RB step to it), on foot in the open world, solo, as a room's host or as a guest. Fire with it places an end (`TetherDirector`, `engine-tether.ts`): the first holds the thing under the reticle (a car, a ragdoll or body, a knockable prop, a box, or a point on a wall or the ground within 80 m; a civilian the crowd draws with no body is given one there and hooked), the second ties it to the thing then under the reticle. It aims along the view's centre, where `TetherArt` draws its ring, also with a free cursor. `MAX_TETHERS` (6) cables at once in all; a seventh drops the oldest. `]` held (D-pad right, the touch Reel) reels the player's own cables in; `Delete` (D-pad left, a middle click, the touch Cut) cuts them. Cables stay when he changes weapon and are cut once he is no longer on foot in the open world. There is no hook from a car. Every cable belongs to the actor who fired it (`TetherSet.owner`), and the host carries out each fire, cut and reel for an actor through `TetherDirector.fireFor(actor, ray)`, `cutFor(actor)` and `reelFor(actor, on)`, its own player's too; a guest aims its own reticle and sends the fire, cut and reel as acts (kinds 2 to 5, through `sendAct`), so the host holds every cable. Controls in [`CONTROLS.md`](CONTROLS.md).

The hook takes any drawn person; the reticle lights them all and each ends as a roped dummy at its drawn spot. A civilian gets a body wherever she is and whatever she does (a counter, a seat, a stoop, a room, an arrest: `OpenWorldBodies.stand` skips the activity, street and building checks for the hook), and the interior she stands in keeps its floor boxes for the physics while the hook holds her (`DoorOpeners.pinned`), however far the eye is. A suspect in a cop's hands, or fleeing, is let out of them: the host logs a `spared` event (`OpenWorldLaw.releaseSuspect`) and every peer's law lets her go at that crowd second (`CrimeScene.hearSpared`, `FootLaw.spare`); the arrest is broken off, and the society still holds the offence. A foot cop is downed where he stands (`CrimeScene.hookCop`, the knock path of a shot or a car: `copDown` is raised for peers, a suspect he held is let go, the player has fought the law and a chase of him opens) and thrown with no speed, and the rope ties to that dummy; he is up and about again after `COP_DOWN_SECONDS` whether or not the rope holds the dummy. A kneeling medic is thrown the same way and not drawn at that scene again; the law takes no notice of him. A guest still sees a hooked civilian at her crowd spot: the bodies are the host's. The interior's floor boxes are handed to the physics before her body is stood (`InteriorSet.holdFloorAt`), so a body 25 to 80 m off does not sink to the street. The dummy thrown for a cop goes when he gets up and is drawn again (`COP_DOWN_SECONDS`), a medic's when the ambulance has left, and a roped dummy is the last the pool gives a new throw's slot to. The sight line looks through a facade to a person when a built room's own meshes lie right behind it: through a window's opening (the glass is passed) she is hooked, through a solid wall of the room she is not, and a facade with no room built is a wall (`FACADE_WALL_M`). The reticle lights only what the fire can take: no cat or dog (hooking animals is out of scope; their meshes are passed through), and no civilian while every body is roped, pinned or down (`OpenWorldBodies.canStand`, which asks the pool for a free slot as `give` does).

In a room every player on foot is seen by everyone: a guest's walker is his own machine's and rides `MSG.foot` to the host, which relays it in `MSG.bodies`; the host's cars, hook and shots take a guest's walker (they come back as `struck` and `roped` on his record: `OnFootDirector.knocked`, `dragged`), and a guest's machine never throws its own walker for a car it draws. A guest still has no fists, guns or hook (`OnFootHost.guest`).

Every machine draws the other players' weapons and shots. A player's record (the host's own walker in `MSG.bodies`, a guest's in `MSG.foot`, which the host relays) carries an arms byte (the weapon slot in hand, `WEAPON_ORDER`, and a shot counter that wraps at 32) and the far end of his latest shot (the point his own machine drew its tracer to), 7 bytes a player; while he holds a first hook end it carries that point too, 6 bytes more. `OpenWorldPlayers.readArms` turns the frame last seen into a table, and `RemoteGuns` (`engine-remote-guns.ts`) draws from it for each other player. Where a pool body carries him (within `KEEP_RADIUS` of the eye, seen, and within the bodies' budget: the common case in a street fight) his body's right arm is held out while his record names a gun or the hook (`Walker.holdGun`, as the local walker's) and hangs for fists or while he is down; his weapon's model (`gunModel`, as the local player's) lies in that body's hand along its forearm, and for the flash of a shot along the line from its tip to the shot's far end (`HandGuns.layOnRow`). Where no body carries him (farther than `KEEP_RADIUS`, unseen, or past the budget of 6 bodies on a desktop, 3 on a phone) he is a figure of the crowd's art with no arm to raise: the gun is held a fixed offset from his torso (0.24 m out, 0.3 m ahead), pointing level along his heading, and a shot's flash and tracer are drawn the same. Each time his shot counter changes: one flash at the gun's tip, one tracer to the far end and, within `SHOT_HEARD_M` of the eye, the gun's sound. A counter that jumps by more than one still draws one flash, and a change is a shot only once 0.9 of his weapon's interval (`Gun.every`, `SHOT_SPACING`) has passed since his last drawn one, so a modified guest cannot make a machine flash and sound every frame (the rifle's 8.3 a second at most). A guest holding a first hook end sees its marker, and the cable to be made, at the host's point for it (`OpenWorldDirector.hookHeldAt`, `TetherHost.hostHoldAt`).

## The camera

`OnFootCamera` (`engine/onfoot-camera.ts`, driven by `OnFootDirector.aim`) keeps the one `ChaseCamera` orbit (no second camera system) for its yaw, pitch and zoom, so the drag,
the right stick, mouse look, the wheel and a pinch work as everywhere, and places the eye itself (`WalkerView`, `walker-camera.ts`), as GTA's on-foot camera does
(`.extraResearch/third-person-camera-and-controls.md`):

- **Follow** (the default): the pivot at his head (the focus 0.55 m over his torso's centre, about 1.68 m up, followed critically damped: 0.3 s walking, 0.12 s running, his
  height with 0.35 s), a boom of 1.7 + 2.0 cos(pitch) m (3.7 m level, shorter looking down or up) at 0.22 rad from above, him in the middle across and lower in the frame with
  his whole body in view: an 80° view across, its vertical held between 48° and 60° (`followFov`). From a run to a sprint the boom grows by up to 15 % (`sprintBoom`,
  Pignole's distance growing with forward speed; the share tuned by feel); the aim's shot keeps its own boom.
- **Over the shoulder**: zooming in moves the pivot out to his right, all 0.5 m of it from a 1.6 m boom in (eased between, so the wheel and a pinch glide through it).
- **Armed**: with a gun or the hook in hand (anything but fists) in the follow view, the pivot stands 0.7 m to the camera's right and 0.1 m up (`ARMED_ACROSS`, `ARMED_UP`), at the follow boom, so his body is off the view's centre and the weapon and the centre dot's target are clear of it; fists, a car or another scene bring it back to the middle. It eases in and out critically damped (`ARMED_TIME` 0.25 s) with no step and no overshoot; from the shoulder stop in it adds nothing, and first person is unchanged. Held aim eases in over 0.15 s (`AIM_TIME`) from wherever the eye stands, to its 1.6 m boom and 0.95 m out. The shoulder side keeps the walls' clearance (the ball sweeps out to the side). The aim ray is still the ray through the view's centre.
- **His own eyes**: inside 0.8 m (his shoulders' breadth and the ball off his head), at his head's height over his drawn torso (eased over 0.12 s, so the gait's bob is soft and a
  stair is not lagged), 0.1 m ahead of the head's centre unless a wall is nearer, looking where the orbit looks, up and down to 74°. The pool's mesh draws his head and torso to no
  camera then (`DummyMesh.viewHidden`: the walker's slot discards the fragments of skin riding the torso and head more than other parts), while his shadow keeps them. Down or
  getting up he is seen from behind again.
- **Walls**: a ball of 0.2 m goes from his head (inside him, so clear) to the pivot, out to the side and back along the boom through the dummies' world
  (`RagdollSystem.sightClear`: the fixed solids and the cars' proxies there, the very walls, rooms (`setFixedBoxes`), facades and stairs he walks among), and the eye stands 2 cm
  short of the first it touches: pulled in at once, let out over 0.24 s (GTA's 0.92 per 1/50 s). However tight the spot the eye never stands in a wall: pulled in within 0.4 m of
  his head it hides his head and torso as his eyes do, and the near plane comes in to the eye's distance from his head less 0.3 m (not under 0.05 m).
- **Recentre**: only a pad's: while the left stick walks him faster than 1 m/s, 1 s without look input brings the view back behind him when his facing is within 0.9 rad of
  straight away from it (0.4 s damped yaw, slower the farther round: it does not chase a sidestep). The keys and the mouse never have it come back by itself.
- **View and the wheel**: View (V, C, T, the pad's B, the HUD's View) steps follow, shoulder, his eyes, and round; at the wheel it cycles the drive views as ever. The wheel
  (its line and page modes read as the same notch) and a pinch zoom on a level boom from 0.7 m (his eyes) out, the shown boom gliding to it over 0.1 s; a notch in while a wall
  holds the eye in zooms from where the eye stands, so the wheel has no dead stretch. The car's zoom range, orbit, pitch range and near plane are kept when he gets out and given
  back when he sits down again.

Stepping out takes the camera from the car's chase over 0.3 s (the eye and the point it looks at blend). While he climbs back in the focus moves to the car and the camera draws
back round it on the orbit (`FOLLOW_RADIUS` 4.2 m, 35 % further), and sitting down hands over to the car's chase cam.

## Seats and doors (open world)

A car has two doors and up to four seats (`car-seats.ts`: a front row on every style, a rear row where `BodyStyle.seats.rear` is set, so every style but the pickup; seat = row x 2 + side, side 0 the driver's). `SeatTable` (`scenes/seat-table.ts`) is the one truth of who sits where; the host's `OpenWorldSeats` carries out `getIn(actor, car)` and `getOut(actor)` for every player, the host's own and a solo one included (`OnFootRide` asks it; a guest sends `ACT.getIn` / `ACT.getOut` and waits for `MSG.seats`). A passenger stays seated through a crash; a passenger's throttle with the driver's seat empty is a get-in that re-seats him at the wheel. Outside the open world the old path stays: his own car's driver door only, nothing written to a car.
