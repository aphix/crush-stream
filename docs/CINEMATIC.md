# Cinematic FX

Visual and camera effects that read sim state only. Nothing here feeds back into the physics, so the
physics tests are unchanged. The only sim-side effects are the clock's: the hit-stop, which scales the sim clock
(`timeWarp`) for 0.09 s on the driven car's big hits, the same way slow-mo already does. Neither snaps: both ease (see the rows below).

**Tiers.** Choose them with HUD Playback → FX `off / minimal / low / high / ultra / Auto`, the `F` key (cycles), or `?fx=off|minimal|low|high|ultra` in the URL (for bench A/B). The default is Auto (Defaults restores it); the HUD shows the current tier and whether Auto is on. The quality knobs sit beside the tier: Draw distance, Render scale and Shadows (HUD Playback, or `?draw=` / `?scale=` / `?shadow=` in the URL for bench A/B; [`CONTROLS.md`](CONTROLS.md)), each Auto by default, and none of them turns the tier's Auto off.

**Ultra** (`present/ultra/`, manual only: `AutoFx` never lifts to it, and a pick turns Auto off). It runs the `high` post chain (the same composite) and adds a photographic look: an HDRI sky (`public/ultra/sky.hdr`) as the environment, turned so its sun lies where the sun light does; the sun's shadow on a 2048 map with a texel-derived normal bias and a wider soft edge (radius 3); and PBR sets (colour, normals at 2×, occlusion at 2×, roughness) over the asphalt, concrete and natural ground. It is a lazy chunk: the code (4 kB) and 3.4 MB of images load on the first pick (the button reads "loading…"; a failed fetch logs and leaves the tier; a phone or software rasterizer loads the 512² image set from `public/ultra/512/` instead: 12 MiB of textures instead of 48, 0.4 MB fetched instead of 2.0). Each photograph uploads as it loads (`renderer.initTexture`) and its decoded picture is closed at once, so the GPU holds the only copy: all nine are resident from the load (the 48 MiB, or 12 on a phone), with no 36 MiB of decoded pictures kept beside them (the renderer process grew 26 MB at the pick instead of 57: five fresh pages each, headless Chromium, RTX 4080 laptop). The first switch links ten more programs (the dressed surfaces): the longest frame in a live city race was 0.77–1.0 s (three fresh pages), the second switch no longer than a normal frame. Per frame it draws the same calls and triangles as `high`, and its GPU cost against `high` is not resolved from zero (24 paired rounds on an RTX 4080 laptop: −0.2 ± 0.5 ms). All of it is CC0 from Poly Haven; sources are in the header of `present/ultra/ultra.ts`.

**Auto** (`present/auto-fx.ts` `AutoFx`, applied per frame by `fxFrame`). One policy for the whole session, boot to race to reel:
- Load check: boot starts on `minimal`. Every device whose GPU is known and is not a software rasterizer runs it (SwiftShader, llvmpipe, softpipe and the Basic Render Driver do not): once `ready` resolves it skips 1.5 s, then judges 1 s windows: the first with room lifts to `high`; three in a row without room settle on `minimal` (a load burst that ends sooner does not). Nothing waits on it. A phone gets it too (it stayed on `minimal` for good while only a fine-pointer desktop was checked).
- Device class (`present/auto-fx.ts` `deviceClass`, the one place it is decided; `Engine` reads it once from the GL renderer, `(pointer: fine)` and `navigator.userAgentData.mobile`): `software` (no GPU name, or a software rasterizer: Auto stays on `minimal`), `mobile` (a hardware GPU with a coarse primary pointer or a user agent that says mobile), else `desktop`. Auto's ceiling is `high` on every class: `ultra` is the user's pick everywhere and `AutoFx` never climbs into it (its ladder stops at `high`). What differs by class: a mobile device never hosts a public match (`canHost`, below), starts the distance detail on its nearer rung, and draws the rivals' spray at 2x at most (`RIVAL_SPRAY_MAX_SCALE` in `rival-look.ts`, the one table: a manual Ultra pick on a phone draws it like High, 20.6 MiB a 32-car field instead of 82.7).
- What it reads (`present/frame-work.ts` `FrameWork`): the RENDER, not the sim, against its own budget, whatever the display's refresh, not the frame rate: under vsync the rate is pinned at the refresh whether a frame takes 3 ms or 16 ms. A tier changes the draw and nothing else, and on a phone the frame is mostly sim (cards: 39-100 ms of sim a frame, 5-8 ms of draw), so a window is judged on the draw alone. `Engine.tickInner` times the draw submit (`scene.sync` and `Cinematics.render`: `Engine.renderMs`, the previous frame's, since this one's GPU work is not yet done) inside the frame's whole main-thread ms (`Engine.workMs`: sim, camera, FX and the draw submit); a window's figures are their medians, the sim share is `workMs - renderMs`, and, where the browser has a GPU timer (`present/gpu-timer.ts`, `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2`), the median GPU ms (used when at least 10 % of the window's frames brought a reading). `busy` = the busier of the render median and the GPU median, as a share of `RENDER_BUDGET_MS` = 0.8 of the 60 fps frame (13.3 ms): the top tier costs at most 0.2 of the frame (3.3 ms) more than `minimal` (measured), the room threshold below leaves a quarter of the budget free, and 0.2 / 0.25 = 0.8 is the least budget in which a climb still lands under "too full". Room to spend: `busy` under 0.75. Too full: `busy` over 1. With no GPU reading (Firefox, some phones) the wall rate backs it up with the sim's share taken off it (a frame the sim fills is not the render's to fix): under 50 fps is too full, and room also needs the median wall interval within 57 fps, so a GPU-bound frame only the timer would show still counts through the wall rate. Worked: the owner's desktop card (8 ms of sim, 2 ms of draw, 3 ms of GPU) is `busy` 0.23, room, as it was when the total (10 ms, 0.6) was judged; a phone with a 40 ms sim and a 6 ms draw (0.45) lifts, one with a 14 ms draw (1.05) steps down. The one tightening on a desktop: its GPU median now meets 13.3 ms, not 16.7 ms, as too full. The distance detail (`DetailGovernor`) and `canHost` still judge the main-thread total, sim included, against the whole frame (16.7 ms).
- Monitor: after every switch it skips 1.5 s (the first post-chain frames), then judges 1 s windows. Three windows in a row too full step down one tier: `high` → `low` → `minimal`, and the check starts again on the new tier. A single hitch, however long, or a load spike of two seconds never drops a tier (the recorded race-start burst runs two windows past green + 3 s). A tier that held climbs back one tier at a time after 8 windows in a row with room, never before 20 windows after a step down; a climb dropped again inside 20 windows doubles the streak the next climb needs (up to 64), so a load that comes and goes does not flap. `ultra` is the user's pick: Auto never climbs into it.
- Matches: a race (or a derby) keeps the tier Auto settled on while the map was set up (the race menu is measured like any other screen), so what the player sees before pressing play stays after it. From the grid, through the countdown, until green + 3 s (the race clock, or the derby clock that runs from −4.5 s to green at 0) no window is judged: the green light's first seconds are a burst of first-use load. From then on the monitor runs as usual, and a device that cannot hold the tier steps down after three windows too full. The race's end keeps the tier too.
- A manual pick (a tier button, `F`, or `?fx=`) turns Auto off for the session; the Auto button or Defaults turns it back on. Each switch logs `Crush Stream FX auto: <tier> (last window <n> fps, <n> % of the render budget; render <n> % and sim <n> % of the frame budget)` to the console: `busy` as a share of `RENDER_BUDGET_MS`, then the window's median draw ms and the rest of its main-thread ms (the sim) as shares of the 16.7 ms frame.
- `off`: the renderer draws straight to the canvas. There's no post chain, no mark map, no crash cam, no hit punch, and no tyre smoke from slip. Sparks and glass use the plain look.
- `minimal`: the same straight-to-canvas render as `off` (no HDR target, bloom, grade, vignette, grain or blur; flash, chromatic punch and letterbox need the composite, so they don't show), plus every effect that needs no post: tyre marks (1024² map), tyre smoke and slip sparks, shake and FOV kick, hit-stop, crash-cam cuts. Sparks and glass use the plain look (no streaks, no HDR glow). A fixed-seed capture of `minimal` is identical to `off` (max channel difference 0).
- Every tier's programs and both outputs (canvas, HDR target) are linked and drawn once at boot and when a race course loads, so a tier switch or a race never links a program ([`PERF_HITCH.md`](PERF_HITCH.md)), except the first pick of Ultra (above). The car finish's programs are among them in both states whatever the tier, the finish for the HDR target alone (it draws only at `high` and `ultra`); a switch that changes it queues the same warm-up (`Cinematics.dressCarFinish`), which then finds nothing new to link or draw.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: no shake, flash, chromatic punch, radial blur or crash-cam moves at any tier. The crash-cam cuts still happen but hold still.

## Thrown drivers (ragdolls)
A driver flies out of a car on a disabling hit: through the windshield on a head-on, or through the struck side's front window on a side hit. Nothing is thrown on a rear hit, a scrape, or a hit that leaves the car sound. The dummy is cosmetic and local: no dummy state enters the sim or the snapshots.
- **Where the world runs.** On a race course the dummies' Rapier world (dummies, purses, knocked props, the cars' proxies, `fitProxy`) runs in a module Web Worker (`ragdoll-worker.ts`, driven by `RagdollRemote`); anywhere else (the sandbox, the range, the Lab, a browser without Workers, a worker that fails) it runs in the main thread as before. The mode is picked at a run's first throw or knock. Frames, throws, knocks and each wanted car's cage go in by `postMessage` (cage arrays are transferred), poses come out; the main thread blends the last two steps by `alpha` and draws. No SharedArrayBuffer, no COOP/COEP. The one thing that flows back into the sim is a torso striking another car's pane (`hitGlass`: a shattered pane leaves the cage's contact points). The world never writes to a car and never refits a cage (it reads the sim's own fit, `car.cage`): it reports the strike (`CarProxies.strikes`) and shows the pane in its new state at once in its own proxies (`overlay`). The sim applies the strike `LAG` (2) frames after the frame it was struck in, **in both modes** (`RagdollSystem.applyStruck` inline, `RagdollRemote.post` for the worker), so a clip recorded in one mode replays in the other and a host and client in different modes agree (REPLAY 78). A "frame" is a busy frame (a dummy, purse thing or knocked prop out). In the worker the sim does not run a frame (`RagdollSystem.waiting`) until the answer it is to hear is in; a worker silent for 1.5 s counts as failed and the main thread's world takes over. `launch` (the exit pane, the cover) and `knockProp` keep their sim effects on the main thread, in the step.
- **Trigger** (`ragdoll-trigger.ts` `EjectionWatch`), once per car per run, on the edge where the car is disabled: its drivetrain dies (a derby kill or wreck, a race DNF by damage), or its engine block is packed back past the kill travel of the slider's realistic end (`killTravel(class, 1, ctx)`: the sourced 0.15 m × durability, × the derby scale in a derby). The second edge is a hit that would disable a real car: at the HUD's default realism (0.25) a fleet sedan only dies at 0.45 m of travel and one head-on tops out at 0.36 m, so no single sandbox or race hit ever kills (by design; the sim is untouched). The hit must be head-on or from a side (`impactInward`), and the peak closing speed over the 0.35 s before it must be at least 6 m/s. Everything it reads (drivetrain flag, `engineTravel`, impact, poses, velocities) is replicated, so a netplay client decides the same way as the host.
- **Launch** (`ejection.ts` `launch`; owner 2026-10-20: "seat velocity at the hit + pitched push"): the driver leaves with his seat's velocity as the hit began, the car's centre velocity plus its turn rate (eased pitch and roll as well as yaw, `EjectionWatch` reads it off the pose step to step) about his point, from the watch's last sample before the hit; then 3 m/s out of the pane along the car's nose, its pitch too (`THROW_OUT`), and 3.5 m/s up (`THROW_UP`). The peak closing over the 0.35 s before decides whether he is thrown, not how. A forced throw (`throwDriverNow`) takes the car's motion now.

## Race tracks
`engine-marks.ts` exports `markMapUniforms`, `applyMarkMap(material)` and `setMarkBounds(minX, minZ, maxX, maxZ)`.
- A track's road, runoff and terrain materials call `applyMarkMap` once. It samples by world xz, so it works on hilly courses.
- `RaceDirector` calls `setMarkBounds(track.bounds…)` on load, and the sandbox rect (±48 m) on exit. Either call also clears the map. The window (`uMarkRect`) follows `CrashEngine.markFocus`, passed through `Cinematics.render` to `SkidMarks.flush` each frame.

## Bench
Run the A/B on the same build with `scripts/bench-browser.mjs --url "<dev url>/?fx=off"` against the default URL (high tier).

The first A/B (2026-10-01, RTX 4080 via d3d12) ran at load average 35–70 from another project. Even the `off` tier swung from 3.6 to 14.8 fps between two back-to-back runs, so those numbers can't resolve the +1.5 ms budget.

The draw-call delta is stable:
- post chain: +11 calls (scene RT, 5 + 4 bloom passes, composite) at high;
- streaks and tyre smoke: +1 each while alive;
- mark stamp: ≤ +1 per frame with slip.

Re-measure on a quiet box.
