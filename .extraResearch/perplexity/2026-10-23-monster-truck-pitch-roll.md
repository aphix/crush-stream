> Question: Monster truck body pitch under hard throttle (squat) and braking (dive) in degrees, and roll angle in a hard turn, as seen in Monster Jam racing or freestyle. How quickly does the body settle after a big jump landing: how many oscillations, and over roughly how many seconds?

There are no reliable published measurements for Monster Jam body pitch/roll angles or landing-settle time, so the following are **engineering-scale estimates from video and typical truck geometry**, not official specifications.

| Maneuver | Approximate body angle |
|---|---:|
| Hard acceleration squat | **2–5° nose-up**; roughly **5–8°** during a pronounced wheelie or traction event |
| Hard braking dive | **2–4° nose-down**; occasionally **5–7°** with aggressive braking or a rough landing |
| Hard flat turn, sustained roll | **3–7°** toward the outside of the turn |
| Very aggressive turn, uneven surface, or partially airborne truck | **8–12°**, sometimes more momentarily |

These angles refer to the **chassis/body relative to the local ground**, not the axle or wheels. A Monster Jam truck’s roughly 30-inch suspension travel, four-link suspension, nitrogen-charged shocks, and large flexible tires allow substantial wheel movement without the body moving by an equally large angle.[1][2][8]

After a large, reasonably symmetrical jump landing, a practical estimate is:

- **First major compression:** about **0.15–0.30 s** after touchdown.
- **First rebound:** roughly **0.25–0.50 s**.
- **Settled enough for normal control:** usually **0.8–1.5 s**.
- **Visible oscillations:** typically **1–2 major up/down cycles**; a harsh, uneven, nose-first, or sideways landing may produce **2–4 smaller cycles** over approximately **1.5–3 s**.

A clean landing may appear to settle in under a second, while freestyle landings involving one-wheel, nose, tail, or diagonal contact can keep pitching and rolling for several seconds. The shocks are specifically intended to prevent repeated bouncing, and the trucks use multiple long-travel, nitrogen/oil dampers at each corner.[1][2] The apparent body motion can also be misleading: tire sidewall compression and axle travel may absorb much of the impact while the cab and frame move comparatively little.[2]

For a simulation or animation, reasonable starting values would be **3° acceleration squat, 3° braking dive, 5° cornering roll, and a damped response with about 1.5 visible cycles over 1.2 seconds** after a normal hard landing. Use **8–10° transient pitch/roll** for an especially violent freestyle landing.
