> Question: Monster Jam truck suspension: of the roughly 30 inches of total wheel travel per corner, how many inches are bump (compression from static ride height) versus droop (rebound)? What is the static ride height of the chassis / frame rails above the ground in inches? Cite build sheets or specs for Grave Digger, Zombie, Max-D or other Monster Jam trucks where available.

The published Monster Jam specification supports **about 30 inches of total suspension travel at each corner**, but it does **not** publish the split between bump and droop. Therefore, no defensible official figure—such as 15/15, 18/12, or 20/10 inches—can be cited for Grave Digger, Zombie, Max-D, or the fleet generally.

## What is documented

- Monster Jam’s official technical description says the four-link suspension uses nitrogen-charged shocks providing **30 inches of suspension travel**.[1]
- Monster Jam’s official truck-facts sheet likewise specifies shocks with **30 inches of travel**.[2]
- MotorTrend describes the newer trucks as having approximately **30 inches at each corner**, with limiting straps or chains restricting full droop.[3]
- Car and Driver confirms the 30-inch figure and specifically notes that limit straps prevent the axle assemblies from reaching excessive full droop.[4]

## Bump versus droop

“30 inches of travel” is a **total relative wheel movement**, not necessarily 30 inches of shock-stroke split equally around static ride height. The static point depends on:

- shock length and mounting motion ratio;
- spring rate and nitrogen pressure;
- vehicle corner weight;
- bump-stop engagement;
- limit-strap length;
- desired landing and wheelie behavior.

Accordingly, the split must be measured from a particular truck at its specified setup. The available public Monster Jam specifications do not identify the static wheel position, full-bump position, and full-droop position, so the bump/droop allocation cannot be calculated from the published 30-inch number.

A nominal 15 inches bump and 15 inches droop would be only an assumption—not a documented Monster Jam specification.

## Static frame-rail height

No reliable public build sheet or official Monster Jam specification located in the available results gives the **static chassis/frame-rail height above ground** for Grave Digger, Zombie, Max-D, or another current competition truck.

The commonly published **approximately 10.5-foot overall height** is the height of the complete truck, including the body and 66-inch tires; it is not frame-rail ride height.[1][2] The 66-inch tire diameter establishes a tire radius of roughly 33 inches, but it does not determine frame height because the axle, chassis, suspension geometry, tire compression, and body clearance all affect that measurement.

### Bottom line

| Quantity | Publicly supportable value |
|---|---:|
| Total wheel travel per corner | Approximately **30 in**[1][3][4] |
| Bump travel from static ride height | **Not published** |
| Droop travel from static ride height | **Not published** |
| Static frame-rail height above ground | **Not published** |
| 10.5-foot figure | Overall truck height, not frame height[1][2] |

The correct answer is therefore **30 inches total, with the bump/droop split and static frame-rail height undocumented in the cited public specifications**. A precise answer would require a team build sheet, setup sheet, or direct measurement of a specific truck at race trim.
