> Question: In a 56 km/h (35 mph) full-width rigid-barrier frontal crash of a Ford Crown Victoria Police Interceptor (or Ford Police Interceptor Utility / Dodge Charger Pursuit) fitted with a steel police push bumper (Setina PB400/PB450L, Go Rhino 5038), how does the push bar deform: does it bend or fold back into the grille and bumper, how far (mm of its ~90 mm standoff) before the car's own bumper beam takes load, and at what load (kN)? Are these bars built frame-mounted and 'airbag compatible' so they do not stiffen the front crush (FMVSS 208 / Ford police push-bumper guidance, Setina crash test claims)? Cite sources; say which figures are published and which are estimates.

There is **no published, model-specific crash-test dataset** showing that a Setina PB400/PB450L or Go Rhino 5038 bends a specified number of millimetres at a specified force in a 56 km/h full-width rigid-barrier crash. Therefore, neither “folds back by \(x\) mm” nor “the vehicle bumper takes over at \(y\) kN” can be stated as measured facts.

## What can be established

- These products are generally **rigid steel push-bumper assemblies**, mounted through vehicle-specific brackets to structural front-end/frame attachment points—not simply attached to the plastic fascia.
- The approximately 90 mm stand-off is a geometric clearance, **not a published crush stroke**. In a severe frontal impact, the bar may bend, buckle, rotate, tear brackets, or transmit load into the mounting structure. The mode depends on tube section, welds, bracket geometry, bolt failure, impact height, vehicle model, and crash alignment.
- The published material located for the Go Rhino 5038 identifies it as a 5000-Series bumper for the 2003–2011 Crown Victoria, but does not provide force–deflection or 56 km/h barrier-crash results.[4]
- The available Federal Signal PBX material claims that its product “does not interfere with” the SRS airbag system, but that is a manufacturer product claim—not evidence that every push-bumper installation preserves the vehicle’s original crash pulse or FMVSS 208 compliance.[6][7]

## Airbag and crush-structure implications

“Airbag compatible” should not be interpreted as “does not stiffen the front crush zone.” Ford’s police-vehicle guidance explicitly warns that different push-bumper designs have different deformation characteristics and that, without crash tests of the particular equipped vehicle, some designs could affect airbag-deployment timing.[1][2] Ford’s current police-vehicle warning likewise says that non-original equipment, including push bars, could adversely affect airbag deployment in frontal impacts and that Ford does not test aftermarket equipment for functionality with Ford components.[10]

A later Ford police manual warns that adding or modifying front equipment—including the bumper system, frame, front body structure, tow hooks, and push bar—could affect airbag performance.[8] Installation documentation for a Ford Police Interceptor Utility push bumper also warns that a push bumper may raise the vehicle’s minimum collision speed for airbag deployment.[3]

Thus:

- **Frame/structural mounting:** generally yes, in the sense that the assemblies use substantial structural brackets.
- **Guaranteed preservation of the original crush response:** no published basis.
- **Guaranteed FMVSS 208 compatibility for a specific aftermarket bar and vehicle combination:** no.
- **“Airbag compatible”:** at most a product-specific or installation claim; it does not establish unchanged sensor timing, occupant acceleration, or compliance in a modified vehicle.

## Requested numerical figures

| Quantity | Published figure found | Engineering interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Push-bar stand-off | About 90 mm, as stated in the question | Clearance only; not a validated deformation stroke |
| Bar deformation before the car bumper beam loads | Not published | Cannot determine reliably |
| Load causing bar to bend/fold | Not published for these products | Cannot determine reliably |
| Load transferred to the vehicle bumper beam | Not published | Cannot determine reliably |
| 56 km/h full-width rigid-barrier result | Not published for the listed combinations | No defensible product-specific answer |
| FMVSS 208 compliance with the aftermarket bar installed | Not established by the cited material | Requires a tested vehicle/equipment configuration |

A rough hand estimate based only on the 90 mm clearance would be speculative and potentially misleading: the bar may begin carrying load immediately, while the vehicle bumper beam may engage before the bar visibly reaches the grille. Conversely, local bracket or tube deformation could consume much of the clearance. Without high-speed video, force transducers, deformation measurements, and vehicle acceleration data from the exact configuration, assigning values in millimetres or kilonewtons would be fabrication rather than a published result.
