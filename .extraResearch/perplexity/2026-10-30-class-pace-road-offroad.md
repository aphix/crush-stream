## 1. 1970 muscle car: Chevelle SS 454

A 1970 Chevelle SS 454 has published acceleration figures ranging from **5.4 seconds to 6.1 seconds for 0–100 km/h**, depending on engine specification, gearing, tires, and test method.[8][9] A commonly quoted maximum speed is approximately **215 km/h**, although period-oriented specifications also give values around 125–138 mph, or roughly **201–222 km/h**.[8][9]

A period test reportedly measured approximately **0.90 g on the skidpad**, which is unusually high for a 1970s muscle car and should be treated cautiously because tire technology, test procedure, and exact vehicle specification matter.[9]

**Recommended game values:**

- Top speed on pavement: **215 km/h**
- 0–100 km/h: **5.8 s**
- Pavement grip: **0.90 g**
- 100–0 km/h: **~43–48 m, estimate**
- Dirt/gravel top speed: **140–165 km/h, estimate**
- Dirt/gravel grip: **0.45–0.60 g, estimate**

The dirt penalty should be substantial because the Chevelle is rear-wheel drive, heavy, low, and fitted for paved-road traction rather than loose-surface control. Its high-speed power may still make it competitive on a fast, smooth gravel straight.

## 2. Stock full-size 4×4 pickup: Ford F-150

A tested 2015 F-150 4×4 reached **60 mph in 6.4 seconds**, equivalent to approximately **0–100 km/h in 6.8 seconds** after conversion.[7] The same test recorded **0.76 g on the skidpad** and a **126-foot 60–0-mph braking distance**.[7] The braking result converts to approximately **38.4 m from 96.6 km/h**; estimating the corresponding 100–0-km/h distance gives roughly **41 m**, though this is not a direct measurement.

A Car and Driver F-150 test lists a governed top speed of **104 mph**, or approximately **167 km/h**.[1]

**Recommended game values:**

- Top speed on pavement: **167 km/h**
- 0–100 km/h: **6.8 s**
- Pavement grip: **0.76 g**
- 100–0 km/h: **~41 m, converted estimate**
- Dirt/gravel top speed: **110–125 km/h, estimate**
- Dirt/gravel grip: **0.45–0.57 g, estimate**

A stock F-150 should lose more cornering speed than a purpose-built off-road truck because of its road tires, soft suspension, substantial mass, and relatively high center of gravity. Four-wheel drive improves acceleration on loose surfaces but does not eliminate braking and cornering limitations.

## 3. Lifted off-road pickup or trophy truck: Ford Raptor

The available results do not provide a complete, directly cited Ford Raptor test table with top speed, skidpad grip, and braking from 100 km/h. They do show the relevant performance range for high-output F-150 variants: a tested 2024 F-150 Tremor reached **60 mph in 6.0 seconds**, while an earlier Tremor reached **60 mph in 5.3 seconds**.[5] A 2014 F-150 Tremor recorded **0.75 g on the skidpad** and approximately **185–186 feet from 70–0 mph**, equivalent to roughly **56–57 m from 113 km/h**.[4] Those figures are useful proxies, but they are not Raptor measurements.

**Recommended Raptor values:**

- Top speed on pavement: **172 km/h, estimate**
- 0–100 km/h: **5.5–6.0 s, estimate**
- Pavement grip: **0.70–0.75 g, estimate**
- 100–0 km/h: **~42–47 m, estimate**
- Dirt/gravel top speed: **145–165 km/h, estimate**
- Dirt/gravel grip: **0.55–0.68 g, estimate**

A Raptor should lose relatively little speed on rough gravel because its suspension travel, four-wheel drive, electronic traction control, and off-road tires are designed for that surface. Its disadvantage against the Chevelle on smooth pavement is primarily tire grip, suspension compliance, aerodynamic drag, and mass.

A true trophy truck would be faster than a Raptor over rough desert terrain but could be slower or less stable on paved corners. For a trophy-truck class, reasonable estimates are:

- Pavement top speed: **160–190 km/h**
- Dirt top speed: **150–180 km/h**
- 0–100 km/h: **4.5–6.0 s**
- Dirt cornering grip: **0.55–0.75 g**

These are estimates rather than figures supported by the cited test results.

## 4. Monster Jam monster truck

The available sources do not contain instrumented Monster Jam measurements for acceleration, braking, skidpad grip, or top speed. Monster Jam trucks are built primarily for stadium jumps, wheelies, and short dirt-course events, so normal road-car metrics are also poorly suited to them.

**Recommended game estimates:**

- Top speed on pavement: **110–120 km/h**
- Top speed on prepared dirt: **100–115 km/h**
- 0–100 km/h: **3.5–5.0 s**
- Pavement lateral grip: **0.45–0.60 g**
- Dirt lateral grip: **0.50–0.70 g**
- 100–0 km/h: **60–100 m**

The truck may corner better on compacted dirt than on pavement because its large, flexible tires can conform to the surface and because the chassis is tuned for loose terrain. On pavement, the tall suspension, narrow contact-patch behavior, solid axles, and high center of gravity make it unsuitable for high-g road cornering.

## Mixed tarmac-and-dirt course

For a course with **roughly equal tarmac and dirt**, the likely order is:

1. **Raptor or trophy truck** — fastest overall if the dirt is rough, loose, or jump-heavy.
2. **Chevelle SS 454** — potentially fastest if the course is mostly smooth pavement with only short gravel sections.
3. **Stock F-150 4×4**
4. **Monster Jam truck** — unless the course contains very rough obstacles or jumps specifically favoring its suspension.

A practical balancing estimate for a 2–3 km mixed lap is:

| Vehicle | Estimated lap-time relationship |
|---|---|
| Chevelle SS 454 | Fastest on a **70%+ smooth-tarmac** course |
| Raptor/trophy truck | About **5–15% faster than the Chevelle** on a rough 50/50 mixed course |
| Stock F-150 4×4 | About **5–12% slower than the Raptor** |
| Monster Jam truck | About **10–25% slower than the Raptor** on a normal mixed course; competitive only where jumps and severe roughness dominate |

For game balancing, use surface multipliers rather than treating dirt as a universal fixed percentage:

- **Chevelle:** dirt top-speed multiplier **0.60–0.75**; dirt cornering multiplier **0.50–0.65**
- **Stock F-150:** **0.65–0.75**; **0.60–0.75**
- **Raptor/trophy truck:** **0.80–0.95**; **0.75–0.90**
- **Monster Jam truck:** **0.85–0.95** on prepared dirt; **0.70–0.85** on pavement

The directly measured anchors are the F-150’s **167 km/h top speed, 6.4-second 0–60-mph time, 0.76-g skidpad result, and 126-foot 60–0-mph braking figure**.[1][7] The Chevelle’s acceleration and top-speed figures are published but vary significantly by specification and source.[8][9] Nearly all dirt, Raptor-specific, trophy-truck, Monster Jam, and mixed-lap values above are therefore labeled estimates rather than direct measurements.
