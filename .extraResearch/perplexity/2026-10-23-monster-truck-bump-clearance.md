> Question: Monster truck 4-link suspension with 66-inch tires: what is the minimum clearance in inches between the top of the tire and the underside of the body, bed or chassis at full bump (on the bump stops)? What type and length of bump stops do Monster Jam trucks use, and how is the body mounted so the tires never touch it?

There is **no publicly specified Monster Jam minimum clearance** between the tire and the body, bed, or chassis at full bump. A defensible design target is **at least 2–3 inches of clearance at the closest point**, measured with the tire fully compressed and the axle at maximum bump—but this is an engineering recommendation, not a published Monster Jam specification.

Monster Jam trucks use roughly **30 inches of wheel travel** and 66-inch tires that are about **43 inches wide**. Their suspension uses nitrogen-charged remote-reservoir shocks, generally two shocks per corner, plus **large rubber bump stops** and limiting straps or chains.[1][2] The official public material identifies the bump stops as large rubber units, and Energy Suspension confirms its bump stops were used on Monster Jam trucks, but it does **not disclose their exact diameter, height, or compressed length**.[1][2][9]

The often-cited **76 cm (approximately 30-inch) figure refers to shock length/travel**, not bump-stop length.[8] It should not be interpreted as a 30-inch bump stop.

## How the body avoids tire contact

The body is not the primary structural enclosure around the tire. The driver sits inside a **steel tube safety structure attached to the chassis**, while the fiberglass/composite body panels are mounted separately to the frame or roll-cage structure.[2] In a properly designed truck:

- The wheel openings are made substantially larger than the tire’s swept path.
- The body mounts are fixed to the chassis/roll cage, not to the moving axle.
- Full-bump clearance is checked with the suspension at the bump stops.
- Clearance is checked through the complete steering and suspension range, including axle articulation, tire deflection, and sidewall growth.
- Bump stops limit upward axle travel before the tire can reach the body or chassis.
- Limiting straps control droop; they do not provide full-bump clearance.

Because a 66-inch tire can have its sidewall compress by **12 inches or more during hard landings**, static clearance alone is inadequate.[1] For a custom 4-link build, the correct procedure is to use the actual tire, wheel, air pressure, steering angle, and axle articulation, then establish the closest-point clearance at hard bump. **Three inches is a sensible minimum design margin; less than about two inches leaves little allowance for sidewall bulge, chassis flex, axle wrap, and manufacturing variation.**
