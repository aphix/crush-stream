> Question: In arcade racing games with a nitrous or boost meter (Need for Speed series including Underground, Most Wanted 2005, Hot Pursuit 2010, Heat, Unbound; Burnout series; Forza Horizon has none; Mario Kart; Asphalt; Blur; Split/Second), what happens when the player only TAPS the boost button briefly instead of holding it? Specifically: (1) does a tap fire a fixed minimum burst (a fixed time or a fixed chunk of the meter), or does boost last exactly as long as the button is held? (2) Which games use discrete charges or bars (e.g. NFS Heat/Unbound burst nitrous vs regular nitrous, Asphalt's tap-for-nitro levels) versus a continuous drain while held? (3) Is the minimum burst usually defined in seconds or as meter spent? (4) How do these games handle boost during slow motion or replays? Cite sources (wikis, developer notes, reviews) per game, and say plainly where a detail is not documented.

There is no single rule across these games. **Most traditional nitrous systems drain continuously while the button is held**, so a tap normally produces only a short boost; games with “burst,” “shockwave,” or level-based nitro convert a tap into a discrete activation instead. Public documentation rarely specifies the exact minimum duration in seconds.

## Direct answers to the three questions

1. **Does a tap fire a fixed minimum burst?**  
   - **Traditional NFS and most Burnout boost systems:** usually no. The boost is active for approximately the period the input is held, while the meter drains. A tap produces a short burst.  
   - **Heat/Unbound burst nitrous, Mario Kart items, Blur, Split/Second, and Asphalt’s mode-based nitro:** yes in the broader sense that activation spends a discrete charge or triggers a scripted effect, although the effect may still have internally defined duration.

2. **Which systems are discrete versus continuously draining?**  
   - **Continuous or primarily continuous:** NFS Underground, Underground 2, Most Wanted 2005, Hot Pursuit 2010, regular Heat nitrous, regular Unbound nitrous, and most standard Burnout boost.  
   - **Discrete or partly discrete:** Heat burst nitrous, Unbound burst nitrous, Mario Kart mushrooms/items, Blur power-ups, Split/Second power-play activations, and Asphalt’s nitro levels. Asphalt is hybrid: it has a meter, but button presses select discrete boost modes.

3. **Is the minimum burst defined in seconds or meter spent?**  
   Usually neither is exposed to the player as a simple fixed minimum. Continuous systems are governed by **meter depletion over simulation time**; discrete systems spend **one charge, one item, or a defined meter threshold**. The exact minimum input duration, if any, is generally an implementation detail and is **not documented in the cited public sources**.

## Slow motion and replays

- **Slow motion:** In ordinary gameplay slow-motion features, boost behavior depends on whether the effect slows the simulation clock or merely changes presentation. If the game slows simulation time, a held boost normally remains active while the game’s resource and vehicle physics advance in slow motion; a discrete charge remains spent once activated. The sources found do not document per-game nitrous timer behavior during slow motion.
- **Crash/impact slow motion:** Split/Second and Burnout-style crash sequences often use cinematic slow motion, but that does not establish a special boost rule. No cited source documents a separate nitrous minimum or refund behavior during those sequences.
- **Replays:** Replays generally reproduce recorded gameplay rather than accept live boost input. A boost activation and its resource consumption are therefore replayed as recorded; they are not normally recalculated from a newly held button. The exact replay implementation is not documented for the listed games.

The clearest documented exceptions are NFS Heat/Unbound’s distinction between regular and burst nitrous, NFS Hot Pursuit’s different recharge systems, and Asphalt’s level-based nitro behavior.[1][4][6][7][9] For Underground, Burnout, Mario Kart, Blur, Split/Second, and replay/slow-motion edge cases, the exact minimum burst thresholds are not reliably documented in the available sources.
